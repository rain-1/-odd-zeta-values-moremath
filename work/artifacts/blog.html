The Price of Purity

What a week of human–AI mathematics proved about ζ(5), ζ(3), and their p-adic shadows — no irrationality theorem, and more structure than we knew to ask for.

Euler proved that ζ(2k) is a rational multiple of π2k, so every even zeta value is irrational. For the odd values the state of knowledge is one of the strangest in mathematics: ζ(3) is irrational (Apéry, 1978), and for no single one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), … is irrationality known. The best window results — "at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational" (Zudilin, 2001) — have not been sharpened in twenty-five years.

This is a report on a week-long research program in that territory. Let me say the headline honestly at the outset: we did not prove any new zeta value irrational, and by the end we could prove precise theorems about why our methods cannot. What we found instead is a layer of arithmetic structure in the irrationality machinery itself — new congruence laws with an unexpected character twist, a sharp denominator theorem whose base case dissolves into one classical congruence, closed-form identifications of constants that had resisted description, and a single obstruction — the price of purity — that we met, independently, on three different sides of the subject. Some of it is machine-verified in Lean; all of it carries explicit evidence labels: proved means a complete proof exists, verified means exact computation over a stated finite range (evidence, never proof), identified means a closed form pinned by high-precision computation and then proved or excluded as stated.

I. Sixty seconds of background: how anyone proves a zeta value irrational

Apéry's proof rests on a pair of integer-ish sequences. Let

an = Σk (C(n,k))²(C(n+k,k))²,    and let bn be the companion solution of the same recurrence (n+1)³un+1 = (34n³+51n²+27n+5)un − n³un−1,

normalised so that bn/an → ζ(3). Two miracles conspire. First, |anζ(3) − bn| shrinks like (1+√2)−4n. Second — the arithmetic miracle — the denominators of bn divide dn³, where dn = lcm(1,…,n) ≈ en. Since e³ < (1+√2)⁴, the integers dn³(anζ(3) − bn) tend to zero, and a nonzero integer cannot. Every subsequent result in the field — Rivoal's infinitely many irrational odd values, Zudilin's windows, the Brown–Zudilin approximations to ζ(5) — is a variation on this template: construct linear forms, control growth, control denominators. The whole game is the fight between an analytic rate and an arithmetic budget. Our program studied the arithmetic side of that fight — and found it far more structured than the literature records.

II. The congruence theorems: the second solution has a Lucas law, with a character in it

A classical fact (Gessel, 1982): the Apéry numbers satisfy a Lucas congruence, aap+r ≡ aa·ar (mod p) — the sequence respects base-p digits. Such congruences are well studied for an and its fifteen "sporadic" cousins. For the second solution bn — the one that actually carries ζ(3) — no congruence of Lucas type appears anywhere in the literature. It turns out there is one.

Theorem (second-solution Lucas congruence) proved
For every prime p ≥ 5, every 1 ≤ a < p and 0 ≤ r < p:
p³·bap+r ≡ ba·ar  (mod p).
The proof is elementary and self-contained; a hostile machine referee reconstructed every lemma independently. The stronger "master form" p³bnaq ≡ bqan (mod p³), q = ⌊n/p⌋, holds with floor exactly 3 in exhaustive exact sweeps verified, p ≤ 31, n ≤ 320.

The exponent 3 is the weight — the number of derivatives of ζ hiding in b. We then did what one should always do with a clean law: tried to break it on the other fourteen sporadic families. It broke — on exactly seven of the fifteen — and the repair is the discovery:

Theorem (the χ-twisted descent) proved under five explicit hypotheses; instances proved for the Apéry ζ(3), Apéry ζ(2), Franel and ΣC(n,k)⁴ pairs
For a sporadic pair (A, B) of weight w whose second solution admits a harmonic-monomial weight,
pw·B(ap+r) ≡ χ(p)·B(a)·A(r)  (mod p),
where χ is the quadratic Dirichlet character attached to the sequence's limit — trivial when B/A → (rational)·ζ(w), and the character of conductor 3, 4 or 5 when the limit is the corresponding L-value. The untwisted law fails at exactly the primes with χ(p) = −1; the entire twist enters through one line — the p-divisible layer of a χ-twisted harmonic sum emits a factor χ(p). The master form with the twist holds at floor exactly w across all fifteen families, 28 primes, five base-p digits deep verified, 0 failures.

In the language of Dwork's p-adic theory: Frobenius acts on the rank-two "crystal" attached to such a pair with eigenvalues 1 and χ(p)pw — the unit root and a twisted weight — and these congruences are its elementary shadow, one congruence per graded piece. The unit-root half is classical; the second row is new, and it is the coefficient-level face of a circle of open questions around Beukers–Vlasenko's Dwork-crystal conjectures. A bonus with a story: proving the ζ(3) case forced a hunt for better closed forms, which produced

bn = Σk C(n,k)²C(n+k,k)²·(2H(3)n − H(3)k),   H(3)m = Σj≤m j−3

proved, by six small telescoping certificates — a two-term harmonic weight where the classical formula has a nested alternating sum. We could not find this form anywhere in the literature. The certificates localise "Apéry's miracle" — the cancellation that makes his proof work — into a single line of algebra: m² + (n+1−m)(n+m+1) = (n+1)², and the mysterious constant 5 in his original paper into one boundary evaluation, 5(−1)nC(2n+1,n+1). Several of these statements, including the abstract χ-twisted theorem and two of its instances, are formalised in Lean 4, sorry-free.

III. The denominator theorem: a factor of 12, and a base case that dissolves

The modern frontier for ζ(5) is a family of "cellular integrals" on the moduli space M0,8 constructed by Brown and Zudilin, producing simultaneous approximations I′n = Qnζ(5) − Pn with Qn an explicit double binomial sum. Their paper records, on computational evidence, that dn⁵Pn is an integer. It is not — their own printed value P₂ = 1190161/384 shows it, since d₂⁵ = 32 and 384 = 12·32. The correct law carries a single global factor 12 = 2²·3, and that factor is sharp. (A prior campaign of this program measured this; the present one proved it.)

Theorem (sharp denominators for the ζ(5) approximations, p ≥ 5 part) proved, modulo two explicitly-stated computational identities verified
For every prime p ≥ 5 and every n:   ordp(Pn) ≥ −5⌊logpn⌋ = −ordp(dn⁵).

The proof is a digit-by-digit induction whose only arithmetic inputs are Wolstenholme's congruence and the classical fact Σj<p j−2 ≡ 0 (mod p) — which is exactly why p ≥ 5 is the right hypothesis. The two quarantined inputs are finite decomposition identities (explicit weight-5 harmonic forms over the Brown–Zudilin summand), verified exactly to n = 360 and against the family's recurrence at 748 consecutive values, with the machine-certification status of each stated precisely.

Two features of the proof deserve center stage. The first is where the difficulty finally concentrated. After the general machinery (a proved Lucas law for Qn, a graded harmonic-layer reduction, a carry calculus, and a fibre factorisation lemma whose engine is a sum-of-residues-equals-zero identity), the entire remaining content of the theorem contracted to a single congruence at a single point per prime: n₀ = (p−5)/2 — the zero of the recurrence's midpoint factor 2n+5. There, the relevant region collapses to three corner cells, whose weighted sum equals −14·Σj<p j−2 ≡ 0. A theorem that consumed forty-odd computational campaigns balances, at its nucleus, on one congruence every graduate student knows.

The second feature is that the proof knows why it works. Along the way we proved several obstruction theorems — statements that certain natural proof strategies cannot succeed (the base-case functional factors through a value map that kernel identities cannot move; no representative in any tested letter alphabet can make the estimate cell-wise) — and the final proof threads precisely the needle those no-go results leave open. The negative results were not detours; they were the map.

IV. The identifications: three constants give up their names

The purity defect. The Brown–Zudilin recurrence has three characteristic roots: 592.08…, −0.0844…, and 0.00500… . Here is the fact that reframes the whole ζ(5) problem: the smallest root is smaller than e−5 — an escape ray fast enough, if rational approximations could ride it, to prove ζ(5) irrational on the spot. They cannot: every rational class rides the middle root (that is why the family's "worthiness" exponent is 0.866 < 1, as a prior measurement established over the whole parameter cone). We asked what the minimal-ray class actually is, computed the mixing constant to hundreds of digits, and identified it:

c = −3/π² = 12/(2πi)²  identified & proved — because In = 2I′n + 4ζ(2)În: the fast ray is the un-purified cellular integral itself.

Purifying the approximation — eliminating its ζ(2) content so that an irrationality argument can use it — costs exactly one Tate twist of decay rate. The price of purity is ζ(2), literally. (Two rounds of 600-digit integer-relation hunting had missed this constant, for an instructive reason: a PSLQ basis of periods can never see a reciprocal period.)

A pure Frobenius constant. Computing the Bloch–Vlasenko Frobenius constants of the Brown–Zudilin operator (with a new instrument: the constants are ratios of Stokes constants, which turns a 1/n-converging limit into one converging beyond all orders — 434 digits in seconds, validated by reproducing the published Golyshev–Zagier tables and settling two misprints in the literature at 219 digits) revealed that the primitive part of its first interesting constant is λ₅ = (514/87)·ζ(5), pure — the impurity in the full constant is exactly the decomposable product λ₂λ₃. In the primitive Frobenius basis, the crystal is clean; the impurity that exiles the linear forms is decomposable dressing. No operator with a pure rational multiple of ζ(5) in that slot had been recorded before.

The p-adic Apéry limits. The ratio bn/an converges p-adically along towers n = aps (a consequence of our congruences: exactly three new p-adic digits per level). To what? Not to the Kubota–Leopoldt p-adic zeta value — we excluded any ℚ-affine relation with ζp(3) at large height, with a structural reason: for Apéry's order-3 operator, the Frobenius slots where ζp(3) would live are identically zero. The limits are something better:

limt C(2pt, pt) = 2·exp( −Σm odd ≥ 3 ζp(m)·(2m−2)·pm / (m(1−pm)) )  identified, verified to 30 digits,

and the general tower limit is an explicit expression in such "Kazandzidis limits" — values of the p-adic Γ-function's generating series, carrying the entire tower of odd p-adic zeta values at once. So the mirror symmetry of the subject is stranger than the slogan we started with: archimedean-ly, the Apéry ratio knows ζ(3) alone; p-adically it knows every odd zeta value simultaneously, wrapped in an exponential. Three of the fifteen sporadic families have no archimedean limit at all (their characteristic roots are equal-modulus complex conjugates) — and their p-adic limits exist anyway.

V. The negative space, drawn with a straightedge

A field stuck for twenty-five years deserves to know exactly where its walls are. Ours are now measured, and several are theorems:

  • No pair-irrationality at weight 7. For the M0,10 cellular family (the natural "one of ζ(5), ζ(7) is irrational" machine), the fast sector is excluded by positivity: the primitive form is a sum of two positive terms, one of which demonstrably decays at the middle rate. The worthiness exponent is ≤ 0.34 against the needed 1, and the entire leverage of Rhin–Viola-style parameter groups (measured across the known weight-5 orbit: +0.68 nats) falls a factor of seven short of the +4.63 required. proved/measured
  • The known p-adic irrationality measures are exactly optimal. Building the complete cost ledger for the p-adic (Volkenborn) constructions from first principles — validated by reproducing all three published measures, μ(ζ₂(3)) ≤ 7.17739889…, μ(ζ₃(3)) ≤ 22.28144795…, μ(ζ₂(5)) ≤ 20.34265173…, to every printed digit — and then optimising over the whole parameter cone shows the published values are unbeatable within the family. Two-term forms in 1 and ζp(w) exist only when φ(pr) = 2; at p ≥ 5 the p-adic smallness cancels the archimedean growth to the last digit. The wall at p ∈ {2,3} is rank; at p ≥ 5 it is size. computed over the stated family
  • But the group goes through the mirror. One genuinely positive structural surprise on the way: the Transfer Principle proved — the p-adic Volkenborn forms and the classical archimedean forms built from the same rational function have literally identical rational coefficients, so every Rhin–Viola transfer identity and integrality lemma is automatically p-adic. The permutation-group method of the classical theory (the famous order-1920 group of Rhin–Viola's ζ(3) work) acts on the p-adic side for free — a fact that answers a question posed in the ζ₂(5) literature and that no one appears to have used.

VI. The landscape: one object, one tax, three completions

Here is the conceptual picture the week's results assemble, and the reason they cohere. Behind each of these sequence pairs sits a single object: a piece of the cohomology of a family of varieties — rank two for Apéry's ζ(3), rank three for the ζ(5) family — degenerating at a point of maximal unipotent monodromy, whose limiting extension class has the zeta value as its period. Everything we measured is a shadow of that object in some realisation:

  • the recurrence is its Picard–Fuchs connection (the palindromic characteristic polynomials are its Poincaré duality);
  • the congruence laws are its Frobenius structure — eigenvalues 1 and χ(p)pw, graded piece by graded piece, with a conservation law r = 1 + ⌊m/2⌋ verified on four families, including a successful blind prediction at weight 7 tying the recurrence order to the unipotency;
  • the denominators are its integral lattices (the factor 12 = 2²·3 is a lattice index times a Bernoulli constant — and, an honest open wrinkle, the geometric mechanism provably cannot produce the 3, whose origin is currently underived);
  • the Apéry limits are its periods — archimedean on one side, Γp-towers on the other.

And across every realisation runs the same tax. Archimedean-ly it is the middle-root exile: the fast ray exists but carries ζ(2)-impure classes, and purification costs precisely −3/π² of structure. p-adically it is the rank wall: every operator that eliminates the unwanted zeta value eliminates the wanted one too (tested against three hundred candidate eliminations, with clean positive controls). Even our own computational certificates hit an isomorphic obstruction: every strategy for decomposing a key telescoping problem failed for one reason — the cheap structure lives only in the combination, and is destroyed by any decomposition. Purity and tractability anti-align, in ℝ, in ℚp, and in the machinery itself. Apéry's 1978 proof, in this language, is the unique known point where the tax vanishes: at rank two there is no middle ray, so purity is free — and that is why ζ(3) fell and its siblings did not.

Is that a theorem? Not yet — and the two cleanest open problems the program leaves behind would make it one. In the p-adic ledger they are called rule R2 and the kernel inclusion ker ρw′ ⊆ ker ρw; proved, they would turn the rank wall into a theorem that is the exact p-adic analogue of why "one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11)" has never been sharpened — one statement, both sides of the mirror. That, and the underived factor 3, and the bridge from O(1)-per-prime congruence structure to the eδn arithmetic that irrationality demands, are where we would dig next.

VII. Coda: how it was done, in one paragraph

This was a collaboration between a human mathematician (direction, taste, adversarial review) and an orchestrated fleet of AI reasoning agents (computation, proof, formalisation, refereeing), under a discipline the prior campaign established: exact arithmetic only; every claim tagged proved / verified / conjectured; finite checks are evidence, never proof; every proof refereed hostilely before being believed; and every beautiful claim stated with the computation that would kill it. Six times a lovely story died by measurement — a sign pattern, a depth obstruction, a p-adic slogan — and each time the corrected statement was stronger than the poetry it replaced. The theorems above survived that process; several are machine-verified end to end; and the ones that are merely verified say so, with their ranges. The papers, the Lean code, and the complete worklogs — including the retractions — are the record.

No new irrational numbers. A new understanding of why — precise enough that "why" now has theorems, constants, and two open lemmas with names. In this field, that is what progress has always looked like the year before someone's miracle.
