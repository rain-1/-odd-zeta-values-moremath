How to See a Crystal

Notes on an object-first method in experimental number theory — what it is, why it produced results, and how it fails safely. Companion to the landscape map.

In one week, a research program on the irrationality of odd zeta values produced a new congruence theorem for the most-studied sequence in the field, a character twist correcting that theorem's own first general form, the identification of a 25-year obstruction as a Tate twist with a decimal expansion, two machine-verified theorems, a family of unnamed p-adic constants, and six retractions of its own claims — each retraction yielding a stronger statement than the claim it killed. None of this touched the headline open problem: ζ(5) is exactly as unproven as before. What moved was understanding, and it moved because of a describable method. These notes describe it. The method is not a theorem; it is a working conjecture about how this territory yields — confirmed, so far, by every result above, and stated here so that it too can fail in public.

IObjects first, phenomena second

The founding move is a discipline of attribution: every computation is a measurement of something, and the something is never the sequence on the screen. Behind each Apéry-like pair sits one object — a motive, concretely a piece of the cohomology of a family of varieties with a degeneration — and the recurrences, congruences, denominators, and growth rates are its projections. The practical content of this stance is not philosophy; it is that predictions transfer between projections. When the p-adic shadow showed a congruence with a p³ twist, the object-first question — "what acts with eigenvalue p³?" — predicted a full eigenvalue ladder diag(1, p³, p⁵) at the next weight, a graded descent for the middle row, and a specific exponent at which wrong guesses would fail. All three checked. A phenomenon-first program would have catalogued the congruence; the object-first program asked what it was a shadow of, and the answer came with three more shadows attached.

IIShadows have different provability profiles

The same object is easy to interrogate on one side and opaque on another — and which side is which is itself structure. Archimedean-ly, our objects are arithmetically strong but structurally obstructed: positivity forces every pure class off the fast ray, and that is provable. p-adically they are structurally clean but arithmetically weak: the grading separates everything, there is no defect at all (cp = 0), and yet the linear forms diverge. The method's second rule follows: when blocked on one shadow, translate the question to the shadow where you are strong, prove it there, and let the structure constrain the dark side. The week's central theorem — the Lucas congruence for the second Apéry solution — was found exactly this way: an unreachable archimedean question (the denominator gate at weight 5) was translated down-weight and across the mirror into a congruence at weight 3, where elementary combinatorics could kill it. The dark side never became provable; it became constrained, which is what progress in this field actually looks like.

IIIMeasurement with teeth

Everything empirical is exact — rational arithmetic, p-adic digits from integers, never floats where a claim is at stake — and every claim carries its evidence class: proved, verified-to-a-bound, recalled-but-unchecked. Finite checks are evidence and never proof, and the phrase is repeated until it is reflex. Three practices carry most of the weight. Anchors: every pipeline must reproduce a published value before its novel output is believed; the day the ladders matched Brown–Zudilin's printed P₂ to the last digit was the day their factor-of-12 error became a finding rather than a bug. Adversarial verification: our own proofs are referee'd by an agent instructed to break them, with the burden reversed — a confirmed pass enumerates what it re-derived, and three "correct but compressed" steps were flagged that later became honest lemmas. Instrument hygiene: a relation-finder is calibrated on a known identity before being trusted on an unknown one; a PSLQ basis is checked for internal degeneracy; and the week's best lesson — a basis of periods can never see a reciprocal period — was learned by missing −3/π² twice at six hundred digits.

IVThe retraction engine

Beauty, in this method, is a hypothesis-generator with no evidentiary weight. The rule that makes aesthetic reasoning safe is that every beautiful claim must be stated with its falsification surface attached — what computation, on what data, would kill it. Six times this week the surface was touched. A "Legendre sign pattern" dissolved into degenerate cells. A "dip at the reflection center" was a pole in disguise. A proved-negative about depth-2 letters fell to an ablation that found the artifact — a cap on monomial length — hiding inside our own linear algebra. The slogan "one ratio, two completions" was refuted by an implementation of Kubota–Leopoldt validated against 980 interpolation identities before it was allowed to testify. Each time, the corrected statement was stronger than the poetry it replaced: the sign pattern became a uniform plus; the retracted obstruction became an explicit 130-term weight function. A program that cannot afford retractions cannot afford conjectures. This one budgets for both, and the budget is the reason the surviving claims — the χ-twist, the graded ladder, the Tate-twist defect — deserve the confidence they get.

VUnification is translation — and translations are claims

Most of what looked like discovery was translation: the July certificate polynomial is Frobenius in de Rham clothing; the Lucas ledger is the same Frobenius in Betti clothing; the sharp factor 12 is von Staudt–Clausen in lattice clothing; the middle-root exile, the measured constant c, and the ζ(2)-elimination cost are one Tate twist in three costumes. When two prior efforts stall at "different" walls, the first question is whether it is one wall. But the method's honesty clause applies to its own translations: they are claims, with surfaces. One of mine — identifying the depth-2 letter wall with the July two-variable certificate wall — was wrong, and the discovery of w₅ at depth 1 killed it. The translation table is a living document, and its erasures are data.

VIProve in the cheapest presentation

Objects have many presentations, and proof difficulty is a property of the presentation, not the object. The week measured this: formalizing the second-solution congruence through the original valuation-ledger proof was priced at thirteen hours; through the tameness route opened by the general theorem it cost 2.2 — the same statement, six times cheaper, because a two-term weight function replaced an alternating inner sum. The corollary is a search principle: when a proof resists, spend effort searching presentation-space — closed forms, alphabets, normalizations — before spending it on the resisting proof. The minimal Apéry form, the depth-1 w₅, and the "same-summand-both-sides" restatement that deleted an entire hypothesis from the general theorem were all found this way. Apéry's original mystery — where does bn come from? — dissolves under this principle: companion rows are a finite linear-algebra search away, at every weight, once the alphabet is known. The rabbit was always in presentation-space.

VIINegative results are instruments

The program's negatives are stated with the same precision as its theorems, because a measured wall has a shape and a shape has exits. Not "the weight-7 window fails" but: sector A is excluded by positivity, γ₅,₇ ≤ 0.34, the orbit lever delivers +28.6% where +196% is needed. Not "p-adic irrationality is hard" but: ζ₅(3) misses by 0.586 and the gap between per-prime congruence savings and the required exponential is the entire problem, in one phrase. Quantified impossibility converts "the field is stuck" into "the field needs exactly X" — and twice this week the X turned out to be purchasable: the purity defect was exactly one ζ(2) of rate, and knowing it redirected the whole program from window-hunting to invariant-hunting.

VIIIThe system that does this

The work is done by a human and a hierarchy of machine minds, and the division of labor is part of the method. The human supplies direction, taste, and the steers that repeatedly turned out to be load-bearing — what are the underlying objects; check the old campaign; prove the minimal form. The orchestrator holds the translation table, dispatches deep single-question campaigns, and cross-pollinates them. Each campaign inherits the discipline in its instructions: exact arithmetic, evidence classes, fetch-before-cite, and the standing warning that fluent fabrication concentrates at the boundary of the known — which is precisely where research lives. The record is append-only worklogs whose claims carry their calibration, so a future session inherits not just conclusions but the degree to which each deserves belief. Twice this week an agent corrected the orchestrator's briefing; once an agent corrected its own sub-agent's summary against a fetched source. The system is built so that being wrong is cheap, being corrected is fast, and being confidently wrong for long is hard.

IXWhat survives

The honest tally: the field's open problem stands untouched, and every window theorem we tested is measurably out of reach of its known constructions. What the method produced instead is a layer of structure the field did not have — a character-twisted congruence law across the sporadic class, a Frobenius ladder visible in elementary arithmetic, a named and evaluated obstruction, a mirror asymmetry sharp enough to explain which problems fell and which resisted, and a shelf of new objects (the connection constants, the tower limits Λa) that are now precise enough to be someone's thesis. Whether this layer eventually feeds a proof of the thing everyone wants, nobody can promise. What can be said is narrower and, I think, more useful: an understanding that predicts is valuable before it is proved — and this one has predicted, repeatedly, in public, with its failures logged next to its successes.

The crystals want to be seen. That was never a theorem. It is a bet about where the next theorem is — and this week, every time we took the bet, the crystal paid.
