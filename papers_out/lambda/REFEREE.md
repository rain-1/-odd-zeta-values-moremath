## CRITICAL

**C-1 (§3.5 "What the theorem says, in words", ~line 712; and Problem 7.1,
~line 1165). The paper's headline slogan is stated for the wrong objects and is
not implied by its own theorem.**

The text asserts: *"modulo the elementary group generated by ℚ^× and the
Γ_p-values at positive integers, the Kazandzidis limits satisfy the binomial
relations … and, under Hypothesis 3.7, nothing else."* Theorem 3.6 is a statement
about the tower Betas: it controls when `∏ β_p^{n_i} = 1`. The step to the
`c_p` requires knowing when `∏ c_p^{n_i} ∈ ℰ_p`, i.e. when `∏ β_p^{n_i} ∈ ℰ_p`
— and `ℰ_p ∩ (1+pℤ_p) ⊇ ℚ^× ∩ (1+pℤ_p)`, which is enormous. So "nothing else"
for the `c_p` modulo `ℰ_p` does **not** follow, under (IND_p) or otherwise.
Problem 7.1 repeats the error ("a complete determination of the relations among
the `c_p(A,B)` modulo `ℰ_p`").

*Fix.* State both places for the tower Betas, and record the correct `c_p`-level
statement, which is: the only relations in which the transcendental factor
cancels identically — i.e. `∏ c_p^{n_i} = ∏ E_p^{n_i}` — are the binomial ones.
Add an explicit remark that the containment `∏c_p^{n_i} ∈ ℰ_p` is a strictly
weaker condition which the theorem does *not* control.

## MAJOR

**M-1 (§6.3 "What is imported", ~line 1147).** *"Theorem 3.6 now supplies a
mechanism for the first group of exclusions"* — Theorem 3.6 says nothing about
`Λ_a`, which is an infinite series of blocks, not a finite monomial in tower
Betas. A relation `Λ_a = α + βζ_p(3)` is not of the form `Ĝ(μ)=0`. As written
the sentence lets a theorem do heuristic work.
*Fix:* downgrade to an explicitly heuristic sentence, and say that Theorem 3.6
does not apply to `Λ_a`.

**M-2 (Prop. 2.3 proof, ~line 339).** The proof interchanges `Σ_{k≥1}` and
`Σ_{m≥2}` with no justification. In a non-archimedean field the interchange
needs the double family to be summable. It *is*, but the reason must be given,
and the obvious route (bounding `v_p(ζ_p(m))`) is an unstated import.
*Fix:* derive it from the quoted convergence of (2). Convergence of
`Σ_m (ζ_p(m)/m) y^m` for **all** `y ∈ pℤ_p` is equivalent to
`ε_m + m → ∞`, where `ε_m := v_p(ζ_p(m)/m)`. The `(k,m)` term then has valuation
`≥ ε_m + mk ≥ (ε_m+m) + 2(k−1) → ∞` jointly. Self-contained, no new import.

**M-3 (Def. 2.2, ~line 317).** *"`ĝ` converges because its m-th term has
valuation `≥ m−1−v_p(m)`"* — this silently uses `v_p(ζ_p(m)) ≥ −1`, which is
never stated, never cited, and is *false* as stated in general (the correct
standard bound carries an extra `−v_p(m−1)`).
*Fix:* same as M-2; use `ε_m + m → ∞`.

**M-4 (Thm. 4.6 statement, ~line 880).** The block formula (4.6) uses `u(i,j)`,
which is defined only *inside the proof of Theorem 4.5*. A theorem statement may
not depend on notation introduced in an earlier proof.
*Fix:* move the definition of `u(i,j)` into the statement of Theorem 4.5.

**M-5 (Def. 4.1, ~line 740).** `h_p^{(w)}(a)` is defined for all `w ≥ 2` and the
closed form is attributed to `\cite[Thm.~6.1]{PaperC}`. Paper C's Theorem 6.1 is
stated for `w = 3` only (it is about `H_3`). Citing it for general `w` is a
citation overreach.
*Fix:* either restrict the citation to `w=3` and note that the same one-line
telescoping proof gives every `w ≥ 2`, or state the general case as a
proposition proved here.

**M-6 (§4.3 heading, ~line 947).** The subsection is titled *"Why the algebra is
not finitely generated"* and the remark immediately below it says, correctly,
that we do **not** deduce that. A section heading that contradicts its own
content is an overclaim in the most visible place.
*Fix:* retitle, e.g. "Why no finite set of tower Betas suffices".

**M-7 (Cor. 4.10, ~line 932).** Labelled `[PROVED]` with the hypothesis
`Ã_a ≠ 0` demoted to a parenthesis. That hypothesis is only `[VERIFIED]` on four
cells (p ∈ {5,7}, a ∈ {1,2}) plus `Ã_1` at four primes from Paper C. A reader
scanning theorem headers reads an unconditional result.
*Fix:* put the hypothesis in the statement, not in a parenthetical aside.

**M-8 (Thm. 5.4 proof, ~line 1037).** *"these involve unboundedly many distinct
basis symbols `[ap^i]`, so the image has infinite rank"* — non sequitur as
written: involving many symbols does not imply independence.
*Fix:* use the leading-symbol argument. The elements `δ(ap^i,1)`, `i ≥ 0`, have
strictly increasing maximal support `ap^i` with coefficient `+1`; in any
non-trivial ℤ-combination the largest such symbol survives. Hence independence,
hence infinite rank.

**M-9 (§6.1, ~line 1079, and the Remark at ~line 1128).** *"It excludes relations
of height below roughly 10^13 at p=5."* LLL does not *exclude*; it fails to
find, with an approximation guarantee. The floor `p^{A/m}` is a heuristic, and
the paper says so once, then speaks as though it were a theorem.
*Fix:* state once and consistently: no relation was found below the floor; LLL's
approximation factor means a genuine relation substantially below the floor
would have been found; and this is evidence, not exclusion.

**M-10 (Finding 6.1, ~line 1086).** The planted relation is written
`3ĝ(1) − 7ĝ(2) − y = 0` with `y` undefined.
*Fix:* say `y := 3ĝ(1) − 7ĝ(2)` is adjoined to the basis as a third entry.

**M-11 (§6.3, ~line 1146).** *"across families, no pairwise ℚ-linear relation
among the Λ_1's"* is stated without its range. Paper C's Finding 7.4 is
`p = 7` only, 12–21 digits. Stating a `[VERIFIED]` fact without its range is
exactly the drift the programme's discipline forbids.
*Fix:* add "(at `p=7`, 12–21 certified digits)".

**M-12 (Prop. 3.1, ~line 525).** Stated *"for `c_p` extended as in
Proposition 2.7"*, i.e. on `ℤ[1/p]`, but the proof only treats integer
arguments.
*Fix:* one sentence: clear denominators by (iii) and reduce to the integer case.

## CHECKED AND CLEAN

The following were checked in detail and are correct.

*Mathematics.*
1. **Lemma 2.1.** (i)–(ii) are standard properties of Morita's `Γ_p` for odd `p`
   and are correctly labelled `[IMPORTED]`. (iii) and (iv) follow as stated;
   the deduction `Γ_p'(0) ∈ ℤ_p` from (iii) is valid.
2. **Theorem 2.4, the closed form.** The Morita product was expanded
   independently. The split at `i=t` is right; the tail for `i>t` is
   `T(A,B)`, genuinely independent of `t` (because `⌊Ap^t/p^i⌋ = ⌊A/p^{i−t}⌋`).
   The sign bookkeeping is correct: each `k ∈ [0,t]` contributes
   `(−1)^{(A−B−D)p^k−1} = −1`, and for `k ≥ 1` the reflection `Γ_p(1+y) = −Γ_p(y)`
   contributes a second `−1` which cancels it, leaving exactly one overall `−1`
   from `k=0`. Equation (2.9) is therefore right, and the Cauchy argument
   (factor `k` lies in `1+p^kℤ_p`) is right.
3. **Kummer/valuation claims.** `v_p binom(Ap^t,Bp^t) = v_p binom(A,B)` for all
   `t` is correct (the digit shift changes no carry).
4. **Corollary 2.5.** `Γ_p(2)=1`, `Γ_p(3)=−2`, so `Γ_p(3)/Γ_p(2)^2 = −2` and
   `E_p(2,1)=2`. Correct; it does reproduce Paper C's Cor. 6.4.
5. **Prop. 2.8 (p-homogeneity vs the closed form).** The sign computation was
   redone independently and closes exactly: the two extra `−1`s cancel and the
   `Γ_p` values at `p`-multiples cancel against the distribution relation.
   This is a genuine consistency check on (2.9), not a tautology.
6. **Prop. 3.4 (`im δ = ker m_1`).** Correct, including the induction and the
   corank-1 claim (`m_1` is onto since `m_1([1])=1`).
7. **Lemma 3.5 (moment injectivity).** The real-domination argument is valid and
   the stated "infinitely many `r`" version is what the proof gives.
8. **Theorem 3.6 (Rigidity), as stated for the tower Betas.** (i) is immediate;
   (ii) is valid *given* `Ĝ(μ) ∈ pℤ_p` — which the revised Prop. 2.3 supplies —
   and `exp` injective on `pℤ_p` for odd `p`. The coefficient bound
   `|v_m| ≤ (Σ|c_x|)X^m` is correct.
9. **Theorem 4.5 (`Ã_a` blocks).** The `k ↔ (i,j)` bijection is correct and the
   count checks: `Σ_{i=0}^{s} #{j ≤ ap^i : p∤j} = ap^s`. The uniform bound
   `v_p(u_s(i,j)) ≥ 2i` is correct via Lemma 4.4(ii)–(iii), and the
   three-estimate double-limit argument is valid. The `i=0, j=a` edge case is
   handled by the convention `c_p(a,a)=1`. This is a genuine upgrade of Paper C's
   Finding 6.6 to a theorem, and it covers every `a`, where Paper C had `a=1`.
10. **Theorem 4.6.** The limit of the inner weight is right, including the sign:
    `(−1)^{m−1}` with `m = j'p^{s−i'}` equals `(−1)^{j'−1}` because `p` is odd,
    independently of `s`. The restriction `m ≤ k ⟺ j'p^{−i'} ≤ jp^{−i}` is
    correct. The valuation bound `v_p(w_s) ≥ i' − 2κ + 2` follows from
    Lemma 4.4(i) with `N ≤ 2ap^{i'}`, and the joint tail bound
    `2i + i' − 2κ + 2 → ∞` is enough for the double truncation.
11. **Lemma 4.4.** Both bounds correct; the lower bound's carry-chain argument is
    right, and `κ = ⌊log_p(2a)⌋+2` is large enough for both upper bounds.

## VERDICT

The mathematics is sound and the new content is real: the `γ_p`/`β_p`
repackaging, the rigidity theorem, the `ker δ` lattice, and the upgrade of the
structure theorem from Finding to Theorem for all tower bases are each new
relative to Paper C, and the proofs check out.

The paper is **not publishable as it stands** because of C-1: the slogan the
whole of §3 builds to is asserted for the `c_p` modulo `ℰ_p`, and the theorem
only gives it for the tower Betas. That is a one-paragraph repair, but it is a
repair, not a rewording — the `c_p`-level statement is genuinely out of reach.

M-2/M-3 (the unstated `ζ_p`-valuation input) and M-4 (`u(i,j)` used before
definition) are must-fix. M-1, M-6, M-7, M-9, M-11 are label-discipline defects
of the kind this programme is supposed to catch, and would each individually be
grounds for a referee to send it back.

With C-1 and the twelve MAJOR items addressed, the paper is a clean, correctly
labelled foundations paper of the right size for its content.
