\subsection{The two open lemmas}\label{ss:openlemmas}

Two of the obstructions above are \emph{fair sweeps with clean negatives}, not theorems.
Each rests on a single clean statement which we believe and do not prove. Proving either
would upgrade an obstruction to a theorem, and both look like the sort of statement the
existing literature is equipped to prove.

\begin{openlemma}[rule R2]\label{ol:R2}
\OPENC{} In the family of Definition~\ref{def:family}, the exponent of the primes
$\ell\in(n,2n)$ in the denominator of $\rho_0$ is $c=m+1$ when $m\ge A-1$ and $c=0$
otherwise.

%% REFEREE [R9]: range aligned with the repaired Finding~\ref{find:R1R2}.
\emph{Status:} \VER{$p\in\{2,3,5,7\}$, $r\in\{1,2\}$, $A\le5$, $m\le4$,
$\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$, $n=32,\dots,48$; $0$ violations; single-shift shape}.
\emph{Content:} it is a statement about when the odd primes in $(n,2n)$ cancel in
$\sum_{i,k}r_{i,k}(i)_{m+1}T_{k,i+m+1}$. \emph{Why it matters:} it would make Correction 2
of \S\ref{ss:Emeasured} \PROVED{} rather than measured; and, independently of this paper, it
would sharpen every published $d_n$-bound in the $m\ge2$ range. It is a
Nesterenko/Zudilin-style denominator lemma in the spirit of
\cite[Lemma 16]{Zudilin2004} and \cite[Lemmas 26--29]{LaiSprang2023}, and it looks provable
by their methods. A second, weaker open point is R2 for multi-coset spreads at $p\ge5$,
which \S\ref{ss:map62} assumes rather than measures.
\end{openlemma}

\begin{openlemma}[the kernel inclusion]\label{ol:kernel}
\OPENC{} For the very-well-poised difference module generated by the family
\eqref{eq:conefam} at $p=2$,
\[
 \ker\rho_{w'}\ \subseteq\ \ker\rho_{w}\qquad\text{for }w'<w\text{ of the same parity class},
\]
where the kernels are taken inside the module of polynomial-coefficient difference operators
\eqref{eq:operator}.

\emph{Status:} \EXCL{five searches, $0/300$ kernel vectors keep the wanted coefficient;
positive controls $300/300$; $\FF_q$ with $q=2^{61}-1$; every hit re-verified over $\QQ$}
(Finding~\ref{find:rankwall}). \emph{Why it matters:} it would turn the $\zeta_2(7)$
obstruction into a theorem. \emph{What it is, conceptually:} this is the exact $p$-adic
analogue of the phenomenon that ``one of $\zeta(5),\dots,\zeta(11)$ is irrational'' has never
been sharpened to a single value --- the window is never narrowed. In the archimedean world
that failure has never been explained structurally; here it acquires a precise algebraic
formulation, namely that the coefficients of the very-well-poised family generate a single
irreducible difference module. We regard the availability of that formulation as one of the
more useful by-products of the scan.
\end{openlemma}

We note the asymmetry between the two: Open Lemma~\ref{ol:R2} is a computation waiting to be
done, while Open Lemma~\ref{ol:kernel} is a structural statement whose proof would be of
independent interest.

We keep both, because the refutation is the structural finding of \S\ref{ss:tension} and it
is not visible without the model it refutes.

\subsection{The structural finding: disjoint support}\label{ss:tension}

The equal-degradation model omitted two terms. One is bookkeeping; the other is structural
and, we believe, is the real content of this paper's negative half.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(1) $C_1$ --- and this one is structural.} By
Remark~\ref{rem:asymmetry}, Bel's $p$-adic criterion needs
$\alpha>\mathrm{growth}+\mathrm{denominators}$, whereas the archimedean Rhin--Viola criterion
needs only $C_0>C_2$: archimedeanly, $C_1$ enters the \emph{measure} but never the
\emph{irrationality condition}. So the $p$-adic side pays the coefficient growth. And by
Proposition~\ref{prop:Gisgrowth}, $C_1=0$ holds \emph{exactly} on the degenerate locus where
the numerator bricks overlie the poles --- which is \emph{exactly} where
Proposition~\ref{prop:symzero} gives $\dgr=0$.

\begin{center}\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth}
\textbf{$\dgr>0$ and $C_1=0$ have disjoint support.}
Measured: $C_1/\bud=1.175$ (symmetric), $0.969$ (Rhin--Viola optimum), $1.073$ (the
crossing point) --- each of which alone exceeds the entire available $\dgr/\bud\le0.4378$
(Finding~\ref{find:howmuch}). Moreover $C_1\ge C_0>C_2$ at every direction measured --- with
equality $C_1=C_0=4\log(1+\sqrt2)=3.5255$ at the symmetric point, and strictly at the other
two --- so the $p$-adic requirement $\alpha_p>C_1+C_2$ is more than twice the archimedean
one ($2.17$, $2.63$ and $2.59$ times $C_2$ at the three directions).

This is a genuinely structural obstruction rather than a numerical one. The group saving
$\dgr$ is a \emph{denominator} phenomenon and lives on asymmetric directions; the vanishing
of the archimedean saddle is a \emph{growth} phenomenon and lives on the symmetric locus.
The $p$-adic criterion needs both at once, and they do not overlap. The same tension appears
at $p=2$ (Finding~\ref{find:cone}), where it is not even a near miss: there $\dgr$ is
identically $0$ on the cone and $E$ rises as $G$ falls.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(2) The coset defect.} For $\zeta_p$ at $p\ge5$ a numerator brick
aligns with one coset only, so at the worst coset the aligned length is at most
$\sum\lambda/c_p$, $c_p=\varphi(q_p)/2$. Measured defect at the three directions of
Finding~\ref{find:gatefail}: $0.50$, $0.357$, $0.4286$ of $\sum\nu$.

\begin{remark}[what would have had to be true]\label{rem:whatwouldhave}
The equal-degradation model allowed the $p$-adic smallness ratio to degrade by $16\%$
relative and still win. What the first-principles computation shows is a factor of $3$. That
is not a near miss that a better direction might close; it is a different regime.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What a construction evading the map would need}\label{ss:evade}

The value of a map is that it says where not to walk. Collecting the obstructions, a
construction that produced a new $p$-adic measure would have to violate at least one of the
following, and we state each as a design constraint rather than as a prohibition.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)},leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=5pt]
\item \textbf{Break the disjoint support of $\dgr$ and $C_1$.} One needs a family in which
      the archimedean coefficient growth stays at its minimum $G$ while the direction is
      asymmetric enough to carry a group orbit. Within
      Definition~\ref{def:family} this is impossible, because the vanishing of the saddle
      \emph{is} co-location (Proposition~\ref{prop:Gisgrowth}). A construction with a
      different source of coefficient control --- e.g.\ one whose coefficients are integers
      by construction rather than by clearing denominators, as in cellular-integral
      constructions \cite{Brown2014,BrownZudilin2022} --- would not be bound by this
      argument. Brown's Remark~47 \cite{Brown2026} asks exactly this question from the
      determinant side, and our Corollary~\ref{cor:transfer} says at least that the group
      identities are available to any such construction.

\item \textbf{Escape the criterion's growth term.} Alternatively, keep the family and change
      the criterion: any $p$-adic irrationality criterion that does not charge for
      $\max(|a_n|,|b_n|)$, as the archimedean one does not, would immediately change the
      arithmetic of \eqref{eq:margin}. We are not aware of one, and Bel's lemma is the
      standard tool; but the asymmetry of Remark~\ref{rem:asymmetry} is a property of the
      criterion, not of the numbers.

\item \textbf{Beat the per-coset minimum by $\Omega(n)$ at $p\ge5$.} This is the hatch of
      \S\ref{ss:hatch}. What is needed is a family with
      $v_p\bigl(\sum_jS_n(j/p^r)\bigr)>\min_jv_p(S_n(j/p^r))+\Omega(n)$. The measured
      hyperplane depth is bounded and constant, so the cancellation would have to be of a
      kind that does not correspond to a fixed $\Zp$-combination --- i.e.\ genuinely
      $n$-dependent structure, not a twist.

\item \textbf{Reduce the rank at $p\in\{2,3\}$.} This is Open Lemma~\ref{ol:kernel}. What is
      needed is an elimination with coefficients that are \emph{not} of size $e^{Gn}$ and
      \emph{not} polynomial-coefficient difference operators within the family --- e.g.\ an
      elimination that uses forms from a genuinely different family, so that the module
      argument does not apply. This is what the elimination technique of \cite{FSZ2019} does
      archimedeanly, and \cite{LaiSprang2023} is the $p$-adic version; the obstruction there
      is non-vanishing, not size --- which is precisely why it yields ``one of'' statements
      and not measures.

\item \textbf{Reach the orbit from a $p$-adic base point.} Even granting all of the above,
      the $1920$-element orbit is currently attached to integer parameters, and a genuinely
      $p$-adic base point reaches it only through the quadratic transformation of Open
      Lemma~\ref{ol:bridge}. That lemma is the cheapest missing piece in the entire chain,
      and it is likely a matter of citing the right classical identity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Where we would look next}\label{ss:next}

Three suggestions, in increasing order of ambition.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=4pt]
\item \emph{Prove Open Lemma~\ref{ol:bridge}} and Open Lemma~\ref{ol:R2}. Neither requires
      new ideas; both would convert measured statements into proved ones, and R2 sharpens
      published $d_n$-bounds independently of this paper's subject.

\item \emph{Attack Open Lemma~\ref{ol:kernel}}, which is the $p$-adic shadow of the
      never-sharpened window. A proof would explain, in a precise algebraic sense, why
      ``one of $\zeta(5),\dots,\zeta(11)$'' resists sharpening; that would be of interest
      well beyond the $p$-adic setting.

\item \emph{Leave the family.} Every wall in \S\ref{sec:map} is a wall of
      Definition~\ref{def:family}. The three constraints that generate them --- rank-$1$
      forms come from very-well-poised shapes, coefficient control comes from clearing
      $d_n$, and smallness is a minimum over cosets --- are all properties of hypergeometric
      brick constructions. Cellular integrals \cite{Brown2014,BrownZudilin2022} and the
      determinant criterion \cite{Brown2026} organise coefficient control differently, and
      the Transfer Principle guarantees that any group identity available to them
      archimedeanly is available $p$-adically. That is, we think, the most promising reading
      of the negative results collected here.
\end{enumerate}
