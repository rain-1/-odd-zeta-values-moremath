\section{Reproduction record}\label{app:repro}
%% =====================================================================

Every numerical statement in this paper comes from one of the programs listed below. They
are plain Python~3 (\texttt{fractions}, \texttt{itertools}, with \texttt{sympy} for
factorisation, \texttt{numpy}/\texttt{scipy} for the digamma quadrature in
\eqref{eq:deltadef}, and \texttt{mpmath} for the $50$-digit re-verifications). All
arithmetic reported as exact is exact; floating point appears only in the final evaluation
of $\log p$, $\psi$ and the two integrals of \eqref{eq:deltadef}.

logs: the four inset-cone searches of Finding~\ref{find:rankwall} took $163$\,s ($144$
unknowns), $575$\,s ($210$ unknowns), $126$\,s ($144$), $198$\,s ($144$) and $118$\,s

$p\in\{5,7\}$ up to $n=30$ and is the longest single job; its output is preserved as a log.

\subsection{Reproducing the headline numbers}\label{ss:reprohead}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=4pt]
\item \emph{The three published measures from the tuple alone} (Finding~\ref{find:gate}):
      run \texttt{ledger.py}. Expected output includes
      \texttt{mu <= 7.17739889912418}, \texttt{22.28144795149432},
      \texttt{20.34265173891448}, each tagged \texttt{[MATCH]}.
\item \emph{The Rhin--Viola pipeline validation} (Finding~\ref{find:rvvalidation}): run
      \texttt{g\_group.py}. Expected: $|\Gp|=1920$; at $(18,17,16,19;0,7,31,32)$,
      $\int_0^1\Lambda\,\dd\psi=24.18768530$, $\dgr=20.18768530$, $C_2=29.81231470$,
      $\mu(\zeta(3))\le5.51389063$, orbit of distinct $F$-multisets $=118$; and at the
      symmetric point $\dgr=0$ with orbit size $1$.
\item \emph{The map} (\S\ref{sec:map}): run \texttt{s\_map.py}. Its five blocks are the
      rank-$1$ map, the rank axis, the $p=2$ detail grid, the structural ceiling, and the
      corrected rank-$1$ map; the last reproduces Finding~\ref{find:map2} row for row.
\item \emph{The crossing evaluation} (Finding~\ref{find:gatefail}): run
      \texttt{gate\_dig2.py}. Expected: at the crossing point,
      $\alpha_p-\mathrm{growth}=-28.97$ against a requirement of $+48.46$, shortfall
      $77.43$; and, with $C_1$ ignored and an ideal split, $42.25<48.46$.
\item \emph{The parity law and the anchors} (Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}): run
      \texttt{s\_vwp.py}. Expected: zero $\rho_i$ at $i\in\{1,3\}$, $\{1,2,4\}$,
      $\{1,3,5\}$, $\{1,2,4,6\}$, $\{1,3,5,7\}$ for $M=3,\dots,7$, matching the prediction
      in each case; and LSZ's printed $\rho_{0,3}=768$, $\rho_{1,3}=73728$,
      $\rho_{0,0}=0$, $\rho_{1,0}=-1024$ with $\rho_n=1,96,14944$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Four artefacts and one driver bug, recorded}\label{ss:artefacts}

We record these because a reproducer will meet them, and because suppressing them would
misrepresent the evidence.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{The $E:5\to3$ drop at $M=5$, $n=24$} is a small-$n$ artefact: at
      $n=36,48,60$ the exponent is back to $5$. Reported in \S\ref{ss:optimal}.

\item \textbf{\texttt{s\_record.py} prints \texttt{NEW RECORD}, and this is a finite-$n$
      artefact of $G$, not a record.} The program evaluates $\mu=(G+\mathrm{gain})/\marg$
      with $G$ \emph{measured at $n=24$}, where $\sum\nu$ has not yet converged to its limit
      $M$ (e.g.\ $\sum\nu=3.125$ instead of $3$ at $M=3$). This inflates $G$ and thereby
      deflates $\mu$, producing $6.817$ against the asymptotic $7.177$ for $\zeta_2(3)$ and
      $17.135$ against $20.343$ for $\zeta_2(5)$; the comparison label then fires against the
      published \emph{asymptotic} constants. The mathematically load-bearing line in that
      output is the one immediately above it, \texttt{BEST over the cone}, which reports the
      \emph{argmin} over the inset cone: it is $e=f=(0,\dots,0)$, the symmetric point, in
      both cases. That is the content of sub-computation (i) of \S\ref{ss:optimal}, and it is
      what Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal} claims. The asymptotic re-verification at $50$ digits,
      quoted at the end of \S\ref{ss:optimal}, returns the published constants exactly.

%% REFEREE [R14]: two further printed negatives a reproducer meets were not recorded.
%% (d) g_verify.py block [C] prints a large [BROKEN] banner; (e) verify.py's "predicted E"
%% column for Lai's A_n disagrees with the measured -- and the measured one is the one the
%% paper uses.  Added below, since A.3 exists precisely for what a reproducer will meet.
\item \textbf{\texttt{g\_verify.py} prints \texttt{[BROKEN]}, and this is
      Finding~\ref{find:F3}, not a failure of Finding~\ref{find:transfer}.} Block
      \textup{[C]} of that program shifts the \emph{numerator bricks} by $\theta$ and
      re-tests proportionality across the orbit; it reports $10/40$ and $5/80$ at
      $\theta=\tfrac12,\tfrac15,\tfrac25,\tfrac13$. That is the expected outcome: shifting
      the numerator bricks leaves the very-well-poised family for the LSZ shape class, which
      by Finding~\ref{find:F3} has no internal transfer identity. The positive
      half-integer test is a different deformation ($h_4,h_5\in\ZZ+\tfrac12$) and is run by
      \texttt{g\_padic.py}, which returns $120/120$, $180/180$, $480/480$.

\item \textbf{\texttt{verify.py}'s ``predicted $E$'' column is wrong for Lai's $A_n$}
      (it prints $3$ at $s=0$ and $5$ at $s=1$ against the measured $4$ and $7$). The
      measured values are the correct ones, $E=A+m+1-\epsilon=3s+4$, and they are what
      \S\ref{ss:lszrederiv} and the ledger use; only that one display is at fault.

\item \textbf{A driver bug.} \texttt{s\_record.py} raises a \texttt{ValueError} when it
      formats the comparison line for the two rank-$2$ control scans, which have no published
      $\mu$ to compare against (the string \texttt{"-- (rank 2: weights \{3,5\})"} is passed
      to \texttt{float}). The numerical content of the third scan is printed before the
      exception; the fourth scan does not run. Neither affects any statement in this paper:
      the rank-$2$ families are dealt with in \S\ref{ss:rank}, by \texttt{s\_rank.py}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is \emph{not} reproduced by the code}\label{ss:notreproduced}

The proofs (Theorems~\ref{thm:transfer} and \ref{thm:classification},
Lemma~\ref{lem:rho1}, and Propositions
\ref{prop:deltainsens}, \ref{prop:symzero}, \ref{prop:F1}, \ref{prop:F2},
\ref{prop:linform}, \ref{prop:cosets}, \ref{prop:orbitnonvan}, \ref{prop:whymminus1},
\ref{prop:parity}) are mathematical arguments, checked by hand; the code verifies their
instances but does not establish them. The two open lemmas of \S\ref{ss:openlemmas} are, by
construction, not established by any computation here.
