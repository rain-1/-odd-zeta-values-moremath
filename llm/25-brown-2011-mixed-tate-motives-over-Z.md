# Mixed Tate motives over $\Z$

**Francis Brown**

## Abstract

We prove that the category of mixed Tate motives over $\Z$ is spanned by the motivic fundamental group of $\Pro^1$ minus three points. We prove a conjecture by M. Hoffman which states that every multiple zeta value is a $\Q$-linear combination of $ζ(n_1,..., n_r)$ where $n_i\in \{2,3\}$.

Mixed Tate motives over $\mathbb Z$

# Introduction

Let $\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb Z)$ denote the category of mixed Tate motives unramified over $\mathbb Z$. It is a Tannakian category with Galois group $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$. Let $\mathcal{MT}'(\mathbb Z)$ denote the full Tannakian subcategory generated by the motivic fundamental group of $\mathbb P^1\backslash \{0,1,\infty\}$, and denote its Galois group by $\mathcal{G_{MT'}}$. The following conjecture is well-known:

**Conjecture 1**. The map $\mathcal{G_{MT}}\rightarrow \!\!\!\!\!\rightarrow  \mathcal{G_{MT'}}$ is an isomorphism.

Some consequences of this conjecture are explained in [@Andre], $\S25.5-7$. In particular, it implies a conjecture due to Deligne and Ihara on the outer action of $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)$ on the pro-$\ell$ fundamental group of $\mathbb P^1\backslash \{0,1,\infty\}$. Another consequence is that the periods of $\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb Z)$ are $\mathbb Q[{1\over 2 \pi i }]$-linear combinations of multiple zeta values $$\label{introzetadef}
\zeta(n_1,\ldots, n_r) = \sum_{0< k_1 < \ldots < k_r}  {1\over k_1^{n_1}\ldots k_r^{n_r}}  \quad \hbox{ where }  n_i\geq 1\ ,\  n_r\geq 2 \ .$$

On the other hand, M. Hoffman proposed a conjectural basis for the $\mathbb Q$-vector space spanned by multiple zeta values in [@Hoffman]. The algebraic part of this conjecture is

**Conjecture 2**. Every multiple zeta value $(\ref{introzetadef})$ is a $\mathbb Q$-linear combination of $$\label{Hbasis} \{\zeta(n_1,\ldots, n_r): \hbox{ where } n_1,\ldots, n_r \in \{2,3\}\} \ .$$

In this paper we prove conjectures 1 and 2 using motivic multiple zeta values. These are elements in a certain graded comodule $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ over the affine ring of functions on the prounipotent part of $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$, and are graded versions of the motivic iterated integrals defined in [@GG]. We denote each motivic multiple zeta value by a symbol $$\label{introzmot}
\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(n_1,\ldots, n_r) \quad  \hbox{ where }   n_i \geq 1 \ , \ n_r \geq 2 \ ,$$ and its period is the multiple zeta value $(\ref{introzetadef})$. Note that in our setting $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ is not zero, by contrast with [@GG]. Our main result is the following:

**Theorem 1**. *The set of elements $$\label{intromotHbasis} \{\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(n_1,\ldots, n_r)\ , \hbox{ where  } n_i\in \{2,3\}\}\ ,$$ are a basis of the $\mathbb Q$-vector space of motivic multiple zeta values.*

Since the dimension of the basis $(\ref{intromotHbasis})$ coincides with the known dimension for $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ in each degree, this yields conjecture 1. Conjecture 2 follows from theorem 1 by applying the period map. Both conjectures together imply the following

**Corollary 2**. *The periods of every mixed Tate motive over $\mathbb Z$ are $\mathbb Q[{1\over 2 \pi i}]$-linear combinations of $\zeta(n_1,\ldots, n_r)$, where $n_1,\ldots, n_r\in \{2,3\}$.*

## Outline

The structure of the de Rham realization of $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$ is well-known: there is a split exact sequence $$1 \longrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{G_{MT}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}_m \longrightarrow 1\ ,$$ where $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is a prounipotent group whose Lie algebra is free, generated by one element $\sigma_{2n+1}$ in degree $-2n-1$, for all $n\geq 1$. Let $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}$ denote its graded affine ring over $\mathbb Q$. It is a cofree commutative graded Hopf algebra cogenerated by one element $f_{2n+1}$ in degree $2n+1$, for all $n\geq 1$. Consider the free comodule over $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}$ defined by $$\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}= \mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathbb Q[f_2]\ ,$$ where $f_2$ is in degree $2$, has trivial coaction, and is an artefact to keep track of even Tate twists (since multiple zeta values are real numbers, we need not consider odd Tate twists). In keeping with the usual terminology for multiple zeta values, we refer to the grading on $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ as the weight, which is one half the motivic weight. The motivic multiple zeta values $(\ref{introzmot})$ we shall use are elements of $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ defined by functions on a certain subscheme of the motivic torsor of paths of $\mathbb P^1\backslash \{0,1,\infty\}$ from $0$ to $1$, and depend on some choices. They have a canonical period given by a coefficient in Drinfeld's associator, and the element $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$, which is non-zero in our setting, corresponds to $f_2$.

Let $\mathcal{H}\subseteq \mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ denote the subspace spanned by the motivic multiple zetas. By Ihara, the action of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ on the motivic torsor of paths is determined by its action on the trivial de Rham path from $0$ to $1$. The dual coaction $$\label{introcoaction}
  \Delta:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}$$ can be determined by a formula due to Goncharov [@GG].

Let $\mathcal{H}^{2,3}\subseteq \mathcal{H}$ be the vector subspace spanned by the elements $(\ref{intromotHbasis})$. We define an increasing filtration $F_{\bullet}$ on $\mathcal{H}^{2,3}$, called the level, by the number of arguments $n_i$ in $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(n_1,\ldots, n_m)$ which are equal to $3$. We show that $\mathcal{H}^{2,3}$ and the $F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}$ are stable under the action of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$, and that $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ acts trivially on the $\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell}(\mathcal{H}^{2,3})$. As a consequence, the action of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ on $F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3} / F_{\ell-2} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}$ factors through the abelianization $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}^{ab}$ of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$. By construction, $\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}_N$ is spanned by elements $(\ref{intromotHbasis})$ indexed by the words in the letters $2$ and $3$, with $\ell$ letters $3$, and $m$ letters $2$, where $3\ell + 2 m = N$. Let $(\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3})^{\sim}$ be the vector space generated by the same words. The commutative Lie algebra $\mathrm{Lie}\, \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}^{ab}$ is generated by one element in every degree $-2i-1$ ($i\geq 1$). We compute their actions: $$\label{introDNL} \partial_{N,\ell} :\, \mathrm{gr}^F_\ell \mathcal{H}_N^{2,3} \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{1 <2i+1\leq N}  \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1}\,  \mathcal{H}_{N-2i-1}^{2,3}$$ by constructing maps $$\label{introDNLformal} \partial^{\sim}_{N,\ell} :\, (\mathrm{gr}^F_\ell \mathcal{H}_N^{2,3})^{\sim} \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{1 <2i+1\leq N}  (\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1}\,  \mathcal{H}_{N-2i-1}^{2,3} )^{\sim}\ ,$$ such that the following diagram commutes: $$\begin{array}{ccc}
  (\mathrm{gr}^F_\ell \mathcal{H}_N^{2,3})^{\sim}  &   \longrightarrow&   \bigoplus_{1 <2i+1\leq N}  (\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1}\,  \mathcal{H}_{N-2i-1}^{2,3} )^{\sim} \\
 \downarrow &   &   \downarrow \\
 \mathrm{gr}^F_\ell \mathcal{H}_N^{2,3}   &   \longrightarrow& \bigoplus_{1 <2i+1\leq N}  \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1}\,  \mathcal{H}_{N-2i-1}^{2,3}   \ .
\end{array}$$ Using the explicit formula for the coaction $(\ref{introcoaction})$, we write the maps $\partial^{\sim}_{N,\ell}$ as matrices $M_{N,\ell}$ whose entries are linear combinations of certain rational numbers $c_w \in \mathbb Q,$ where $w$ is a word in $\{2,3\}$ which has a single $3.$ The numbers $c_w$ are defined as follows. We prove that for all $a,b\in \mathbb N$, there exist numbers $\alpha^{a,b}_i\in \mathbb Q$ such that $$\label{introZagmot}
\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(\underbrace{2,\ldots, 2}_{a}, 3,\underbrace{2,\ldots, 2}_{b})= \alpha_n^{a,b} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)+  \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \alpha^{a,b}_i\, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2i+1)  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(\underbrace{2,\ldots, 2}_{n-i})\,   \ .$$ where $n=a+b+1$. For any word $w$ of the form $2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}$, the number $c_w$ is the coefficient $\alpha^{a,b}_{n}$ of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)$ in $(\ref{introZagmot})$. At this point we use a crucial arithmetic result due to Don Zagier [@Zagier], who proved an explicit formula for $\zeta(2,\ldots, 2, 3,2,\ldots, 2)$ in terms of $\zeta(2i+1)$ and powers of $\pi$ of the same shape as $(\ref{introZagmot})$. Since the transcendence conjectures for multiple zeta values are not known, this does not immediately imply a formula for the coefficients $\alpha^{a,b}_i$. However, in $\S\ref{sect4}$ we show how to lift Zagier's theorem from real numbers to motivic multiple zeta values, which yields a precise formula for the coefficients $\alpha^{a,b}_i$, and in particular, $c_w$. From this, we deduce that the $c_w$ satisfy many special $2$-adic properties. By exploiting these properties, we show that the matrices $M_{N,\ell}$, which are rather complicated, are upper-triangular to leading $2$-adic order. From this, we show that the maps $(\ref{introDNL})$ are invertible for $\ell \geq 1$, and theorem $\ref{intromaintheorem}$ follows by an induction on the level. The proof also shows that the level filtration is dual to the filtration induced by the descending central series of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$.

P. Deligne has obtained analogous results in the case $\mathbb P^1\backslash \{0, \mu_N,\infty\}$, where $\mu_N$ is the set of $N^{\mathrm{th}}$ roots of unity, and $N=2,3,4,6$ or $8$ [@D]. His argument uses the depth filtration and proves that it is dual to the filtration induced by the descending central series of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$. Note that in the case $N=1$ this is false, starting from weight 12.

The notes [@Br] might serve as an introduction to this paper.

# Motivic Multiple Zeta Values

## Preliminaries

With the notations of [@DG], §5.13, let ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$ denote the de Rham realization of the motivic torsor of paths on $\mathbb P^1\backslash \{0,1,\infty\}$ from $0$ to $1$ (with tangent vectors $1$, $-1$ respectively). It is the functor which to any $\mathbb Q$-algebra $R$ associates the set ${}_0 \Pi_{1}(R)$ of group-like series in the algebra $R\langle \langle e_0,e_1\rangle\rangle$ of non-commutative formal power series in two generators. Its ring of affine functions over $\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic to $$\label{opi01} \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) \cong \mathbb Q\langle e^0, e^1 \rangle\ ,$$ which is a commutative, graded algebra equipped with the shuffle product. To every word $w$ in the letters $e^0,e^1$ corresponds the function which maps a series $S \in {}_0 \Pi_{1}(R)$ to the coefficient of $w$ (viewed as a word in $e_0,e_1$) in $S$.

Let $dch\in {}_0 \Pi_{1}(\mathbb R)$ denote the de Rham image of the straight line from $0$ to $1$ ([@DG], §5.16). It is a formal power series whose coefficients are multiple zeta values, and is also known as the Drinfeld associator. It defines a function $$\label{dchdual}
 dch: \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})\longrightarrow\mathbb R$$ which maps a word $w$ in $e^0, e^1$ to the coefficient of $w$ in $dch$.

Since ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$ is the de Rham realization of an (ind-) mixed Tate motive over $\mathbb Z$, the group $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$ acts upon it ([@DG], §5.12). The group $\mathcal{G_{MT'}}$ in the introduction is the quotient of $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$ by the kernel of this action. Let $\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}$ denote the corresponding quotient of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$. We shall denote the graded ring of affine functions on $\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}$ over $\mathbb Q$ by $$\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}})\ .$$ The action $\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}\times {}_0 \Pi_{1}\rightarrow {}_0 \Pi_{1}$ of the prounipotent part of $\mathcal{G_{MT'}}$ gives rise to a coaction $$\label{Ocoaction}
  \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) .$$

Now let $A$ denote the group of automorphisms of the de Rham fundamental groupoid of $\mathbb P^1 \backslash \{0,1,\infty\}$ with base points $0,1$ and which respects $e_0\in \mathrm{Lie}\, ({}_0 \Pi_{0})$ and $e_1\in \mathrm{Lie}\, ({}_1 \Pi_{1})$. The action of $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$ on ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$ factors through the action of $A$ on ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$. The latter was computed by Ihara as follows (see [@DG], §5.15). Let ${}_0 1_{1}$ denote the identity element in ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$. There is an isomorphism of schemes $$a\mapsto a .{}_0 1_{1}: A \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} {}_0 \Pi_{1}\ .$$ Via this identification, the action of $A$ on ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$ can be computed explicitly. In particular, its antisymmetrization is given by the Ihara bracket. The dual coaction $$\label{Gonchcoact}
\mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) \overset{\Delta}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) \cong \mathcal{O}(A) \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})$$ was computed by Goncharov in [@GG], Theorem 1.2, except that the two right-hand factors are interchanged. The formula involves $\mathcal{O}( {}_a \Pi_b)$ for all $a,b\in \{0,1\}$ but can easily be rewritten in terms of $\mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})$ only (this is the content of Properties $\mathbf{I0}$, $\mathbf{I1}$, $\mathbf{I3}$ below). It follows that the coaction $(\ref{Ocoaction})$ is obtained by composing $\Delta$ of $(\ref{Gonchcoact})$ with the map $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{acton1}
  \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) & \longrightarrow&  \mathcal{A}\\
\phi & \mapsto &  g \mapsto \phi(g. {}_0 1_{1}) \ , \nonumber

\end{aligned}$$ applied to the left-hand factor of $\mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) \otimes_{\mathbb Q}   \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})$. Note that since $\mathbb{G}_m$ acts trivially on ${}_0 1_{1}$, the map $(\ref{acton1})$ necessarily loses information about the weight grading.

## Definition of motivic MZVs

Let $I \subset \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})$ be the kernel of the map $dch$ $(\ref{dchdual})$. It describes the $\mathbb Q$-linear relations between multiple zeta values. Let $J^{\mathcal{MT}} \subseteq I$ be the largest graded ideal contained in $I$ which is closed under the coaction $(\ref{Ocoaction})$.

**Definition 3**. Define the graded coalgebra of motivic multiple zeta values to be $$\label{Hodef} \mathcal{H}= \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) / J^{\mathcal{MT}} \ .$$ A word $w$ in the letters $0$ and $1$ defines an element in $(\ref{opi01})$. Denote its image in $\mathcal{H}$ by $$\label{defnImot} I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;w;1) \in \mathcal{H}\ ,$$ which we shall call a motivic iterated integral. For $n_0\geq0$ and $n_1,\ldots, n_r \geq 1$, let $$\label{zetamotdefn}
  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_{n_0}(n_1,\ldots, n_r) = I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0; \underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n_0},\underbrace{1,0,\ldots,0}_{n_1}, \ldots,\underbrace{1,0,\ldots,0}_{n_r}  ;1) \ .$$ In the case when $n_0=0$, we shall simply write this $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(n_1,\ldots, n_r)$ and call it a motivic multiple zeta value. As usual, we denote the grading on $\mathcal{H}$ by a subscript.

The coaction $(\ref{Ocoaction})$ induces a coaction $\Delta:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow \mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}$. By the discussion at the end of §2.1, it can be computed from $(\ref{Gonchcoact})$, i.e., the following diagram commutes: $$\label{coprodistherightone}
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1}) & \longrightarrow& \mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})   \\
 \downarrow &   & \downarrow   \\
  \mathcal{H}& \longrightarrow& \mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}\ .
\end{array}$$ Furthermore, the map $dch$ $(\ref{dchdual})$ factors through $\mathcal{H}$. The resulting homomorphism from $\mathcal{H}$ to $\mathbb R$ shall be called the period map, which we denote by $$\label{perdef} per: \mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb R\ .$$

*Remark 4*. The ideal $J^{\mathcal{MT}}$ could be called the ideal of motivic relations. A linear combination $R\subset \mathcal{O}({}_0 \Pi_{1})$ of words is a relation between motivic multiple zetas if

1). $R$ holds numerically (i.e., $per (R)=0$),

2). $R'$ holds numerically for all transforms $R'$ of $R$ under the coaction $(\ref{Ocoaction})$.

This argument is used in $\S 4$ to lift certain relations from multiple zetas to their motivic versions, and can be made into a kind of numerical algorithm (see [@Br]).

## The role of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$

It is also convenient to consider the dual point of view. Let $\mathcal{Y}=\mathrm{Spec} \, \mathcal{H}$. It is the Zariski closure of the $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$-orbit of $dch$, i.e., $$\mathcal{Y}= \overline{\mathcal{G_{MT}}.dch }\ .$$ Thus $\mathcal{Y}$ is a subscheme of the extension of scalars ${}_0 \Pi_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$, but is in fact defined over $\mathbb Q$ since $dch$ is Betti-rational. Let $\tau$ denote the action of $\mathbb{G}_m$ on ${}_0 \Pi_{1}$. The map $\tau(\lambda)$ multiplies elements of degree $d$ by $\lambda^d$. Let us choose a rational point $\gamma \in \mathcal{Y}(\mathbb Q)$ which is even, i.e., $\tau(-1)\gamma=\gamma$ (see [@DG], §5.20). Since $\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}$ is the quotient of $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ through which it acts on $\mathcal{Y}$, we obtain an isomorphism $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{gammamap}
\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}\times \mathbb{A}^1 &\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} & \mathcal{Y}\\
(g,t) & \mapsto & g \, \tau({\sqrt{t}}) . \gamma  \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ The parameter $t$ is retrieved by taking the coefficient of $e_0 e_1$ in the series $g \, \tau({\sqrt{t}}).\gamma$. Thus $(\ref{gammamap})$ gives rise to an isomorphism of graded algebra comodules $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{Hotens}
\mathcal{H}\cong \mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q[\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)] \ ,
\end{aligned}$$ which depends on $\gamma$, where $\Delta(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2))= 1 \otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$. Most of our constructions will not in fact depend on this choice of $\gamma$, but we may fix it once and for all. Since the leading term of $dch$ is ${}_0 1_{1}$, we have $\lim_{t \rightarrow 0} \tau(t) dch= {}_0 1_{1}$, which shows that $$\mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}.{}_0 1_{1}\subseteq \mathcal{Y}\ .$$ Thus the map $\mathcal{H}\rightarrow \mathcal{A}$ is induced by $(\ref{acton1})$, and sends $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ to zero.

**Definition 5**. Let us denote the graded ring of affine functions on $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ over $\mathbb Q$ by $$\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}= \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}})\ ,$$ and define $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}=\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q[f_2],$ where the elements $f_2^k$ are in degree $2k$. Thus $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ is a graded algebra comodule over $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}$, and its grading shall be called the weight hereafter. We shall also denote the coaction by $$\label{homtdelta}
 \Delta: \mathcal{H^{MT_+}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H^{MT_+}}\ .$$ It is uniquely determined by the property $\Delta f_2 = 1\otimes f_2$.

In conclusion, the inclusion $\mathcal{A}\rightarrow \mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}$ which is dual to the quotient map $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}\rightarrow \mathcal{G_{\mathcal{U'}}}$, induces an injective morphism of graded algebra comodules $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{HotoHomt}
\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}
\end{aligned}$$ which sends $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ to $f_2$, by $(\ref{Hotens})$. The map $(\ref{HotoHomt})$ implicitly depends on the choice of $\gamma$. Abusively, we shall sometimes identify motivic multiple zeta values with their images under $(\ref{HotoHomt})$, i.e., as elements in $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$.

## Main properties

In order to write down the formula for the coaction on the motivic iterated integrals, we must slightly extend the notation $(\ref{defnImot})$. For all sequences $a_0,\ldots, a_{n+1} \in \{0,1\}$, there are elements $$\label{Sec2Imotdef}
 I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_1,\ldots,a_n ;a_{n+1}) \in \mathcal{H}_n$$ given by $(\ref{defnImot})$ if $a_0=0$ and $a_{n+1}=1$, and uniquely defined by the properties below.

I0

:   If $n\geq 1$, $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_1,\ldots,a_n; a_{n+1})=0 \hbox{ if } a_0 =a_{n+1}$ or $a_1=\ldots =a_n$ .

I1

:   $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_1)=1$ for all $a_0,a_1\in \{0,1\}$ and $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2) = f_2$.

I2

:   *Shuffle product (special case).* We have for $k\geq0, n_1,\ldots, n_r\geq 1$, $$\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_k(n_1,\ldots, n_r) = (-1)^k   \sum_{i_1+ \ldots +i_r=k} \! \binom{n_1+i_1-1}{i_1}\ldots \binom{n_r+i_r-1}{i_r} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(n_1+i_1,  \ldots, n_r+i_r) \nonumber$$

I3

:   *Reflection formulae*. For all $a_1,\ldots, a_n\in \{0,1\}$, $$I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;a_1,\ldots, a_n;1) = (-1)^n I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;a_n,\ldots, a_1;0) =  I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;1-a_n,\ldots, 1-a_1;1)$$

The motivic multiple zeta values of [@GG] are, up to a possible sign, the images of $(\ref{Sec2Imotdef})$ under the map $\pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow \mathcal{A}$ which sends $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ to zero. We have shown ($(\ref{Gonchcoact})$, $(\ref{coprodistherightone})$):

**Theorem 6**. *The coaction for the motivic multiple zeta values $$\label{fulldelta} \Delta: \mathcal{H}\rightarrow \mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}\ $$ is given by the same formula as [@GG], Theorem 1.2, with the factors interchanged. In particular, if $a_0,\ldots, a_{n+1}\in \{0,1\}$, then $\Delta\, I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_1,\ldots, a_{n};a_{n+1})$ equals $$\label{defGcoproduct}  \sum_{i_0<i_1<  \ldots<i_{k+1} \atop i_0=0, i_{k+1}=n+1}
  \pi \Big( \prod_{p=0}^k  I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_{i_p}; a_{{i_p}+1}, .\, .\,, a_{i_{p+1}-1} ;a_{i_{p+1}}) \Big)\otimes  I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_{i_1},.\, .\,, a_{i_k}; a_{n+1})$$ where the first sum is also over all values of $k$ for $0\leq k\leq n$.*

Lastly, the period map $per: \mathcal{H}\rightarrow \mathbb R$ can be computed as follows: $$per\big( I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_1,\ldots, a_n;a_{n+1} ) \big)=  \int_{a_0}^{a_{n+1}} \omega_{a_1}\ldots \omega_{a_n} \ ,\nonumber$$ where $\omega_{0} = {dt \over t}, \omega_1 = {dt \over 1-t}$, and the right-hand side is a shuffle-regularized iterated integral (e.g., [@DG] §5.16). Note that the sign of $\omega_1$ varies in the literature. Here, the signs are chosen such that the period of the motivic multiple zeta values are $$\label{periodmapsigns}
per  \big( \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(n_1,\ldots, n_r)\big)  =  \zeta(n_1,\ldots, n_r) \quad \hbox{ when } n_r\geq 2\ .$$

## Structure of $\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$

The structure of $\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb Z)$ is determined by the data: $$\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb Z)}^1(\mathbb Q(0),\mathbb Q(n)) \cong \left\{
                           \begin{array}{ll}
                             \mathbb Q\  & \hbox{if } n\geq 3 \hbox{ is odd}\ ,  \nonumber \\
                             0\   & \hbox{otherwise} \ ,
                           \end{array}
                         \right.$$ and the fact that the $\mathrm{Ext}^2$'s vanish. Thus $\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb Z)$ is equivalent to the category of representations of a group scheme $\mathcal{G_{MT}}$ over $\mathbb Q$, which is a semi-direct product $$\mathcal{G_{MT}}\cong  \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}\rtimes \mathbb{G}_m \ ,$$ where $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is a prounipotent group whose Lie algebra $\mathrm{Lie}\, \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ is isomorphic to the free Lie algebra with one generator $\sigma_{2i+1}$ in every degree $-2i-1$, for $i\geq 1$. Let $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}$ be the graded ring of functions on $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$, where the grading is with respect to the action of $\mathbb{G}_m$. Since the degrees of the $\sigma_{2i+1}$ tend to minus infinity, no information is lost in passing to the graded version $(\mathrm{Lie}\, \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}})^{gr}$ of $\mathrm{Lie}\, \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}}$ (Proposition 2.2 $(ii)$ of [@DG]). By the above, $\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}$ is non-canonically isomorphic to the graded dual of the universal envelopping algebra of $(\mathrm{Lie}\, \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{U}})^{gr}$ over $\mathbb Q$. We shall denote this by $$\mathcal{U}' = \mathbb Q\langle f_3,f_5,\ldots \rangle\ .$$ Its underlying vector space has a basis consisting of non-commutative words in symbols $f_{2i+1}$ in degree $2i+1$, and the multiplication is given by the shuffle product $\, \hbox{\rus x} \,$. The coproduct is given by the following deconcatenation formula: $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{Udelta}
 \Delta:\mathcal{U}' & \rightarrow  & \mathcal{U}' \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{U}'  \\
\Delta(f_{i_1}\ldots f_{i_n}) & =  &  \sum_{k=0}^{n} f_{i_1}\ldots f_{i_{k}}\otimes  f_{i_{k+1}}\ldots f_{i_n}  \ , \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ when $n\geq 0$. Let us consider the following universal comodule $$\mathcal{U}=  \mathbb Q\langle f_3,f_5,\ldots \rangle \otimes_{\mathbb Q}  \mathbb Q[f_2]$$ where $f_2$ is of degree $2$, commutes with all generators $f_{2n+1}$ of odd degree, and the coaction $\Delta: \mathcal{U}\rightarrow \mathcal{U}' \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{U}$ satisfies $\Delta(f_2) =1 \otimes f_2$. The degree will also be referred to as the weight. By the above discussion, there exists a non-canonical isomorphism $$\label{HisomU}
\phi:\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}\cong \mathcal{U}$$ of algebra comodules which sends $f_2$ to $f_2$. These notations are useful for explicit computations, for which it can be convenient to vary the choice of map $\phi$ (see [@Br]).

**Lemma 7**. *Let $d_k=\dim \mathcal{U}_k$, where $\mathcal{U}_k$ is the graded piece of $\mathcal{U}$ of weight $k$. Then $$\label{enumeration}
\sum_{k\geq 0} d_k t^k = {1\over  1-t^2-t^3}\ .$$ In particular, $d_0=1, d_1=0, d_2=1$ and $d_k= d_{k-2}+d_{k-3}$ for $k\geq 3$.*

*Proof.* The Poincaré series of $\mathbb Q\langle f_3,f_5, \ldots \rangle$ is given by ${1\over 1-t^3-t^5-\ldots } = {1-t^2 \over 1-t^2-t^3}$. If we multiply by the Poincaré series ${1\over 1-t^2}$ for $\mathbb Q[f_2]$, we obtain $(\ref{enumeration})$. ◻

**Definition 8**. It will be convenient to define an element $f_{2n} \in \mathcal{U}_{2n}$, for $n\geq 2$, by $$f_{2n}=b_n f_2^n \ ,$$where $b_n \in \mathbb Q^{\times}$ is the constant in Euler's relation $\zeta(2n)=b_n \zeta(2)^n$.

Let us denote the Lie coalgebra of indecomposable elements of $\mathcal{U}'$ by $$\label{Lnotcurlydefn}
 L= {\mathcal{U}'_{>0}\over \mathcal{U}'_{>0} \mathcal{U}'_{>0} }\ ,$$ and for any $N\geq 1$, let $\pi_{N}: \mathcal{U}'_{>0}\rightarrow  L_N$ denote the quotient map followed by projection onto the graded part of weight $N$. For any $r\geq 1$, consider the map $$\label{UDr} D_{2r+1}: \mathcal{U}\longrightarrow L_{2r+1} \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{U}$$ defined by composing $\Delta'= \Delta - 1\otimes id$ with $\pi_{2r+1}\otimes id$. Let $$\label{UDltN} D_{<N}=\bigoplus_{1 < 2i+1< N} D_{2i+1} \ .$$

**Lemma 9**. *$(\ker D_{<N}) \cap \mathcal{U}_N = \mathbb Q\, f_N\ .$*

*Proof.* Every element $\xi \in \mathcal{U}_N$ can be uniquely written in the form $$\xi = \sum_{1< 2r+1 < N} f_{2r+1} v_r + c f_N$$ where $c\in \mathbb Q$, $v_r \in \mathcal{U}_{N-2r-1}$, and the multiplication on the right-hand side is the concatenation product. The graded dual of $L$ is isomorphic to the free Lie algebra with generators $f^{\vee}_{2r+1}$ in degrees $-2r-1$ dual to the $f_{2r+1}$. Each element $f^{\vee}_{2r+1}$ defines a map $f^{\vee}_{2r+1}: L \rightarrow \mathbb Q$ which sends $f_{2r+1}$ to $1$. By definition, $$(f^{\vee}_{2r+1} \otimes id )\circ D_{2r+1} \xi = v_r\ ,$$ for all $1<2r+1<N$. It follows immediately that if $\xi$ is in the kernel of $D_{<N}$ then it is of the form $\xi = cf_N$. Since $D_{<N} f_N=0$, the result follows. ◻

# Cogenerators of the coalgebra

## Infinitesimal coaction

In order to simplify the formula $(\ref{defGcoproduct})$, let $$\label{Ldef}
\mathcal{L}= {\mathcal{A}_{>0} \over \mathcal{A}_{>0} \mathcal{A}_{>0}}$$ denote the Lie coalgebra of $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{H}/ f_2\mathcal{H}$, and let $\pi:\mathcal{H}_{>0}\rightarrow \mathcal{L}$ denote the quotient map. Since $\mathcal{L}$ inherits a weight grading from $\mathcal{H}$, let $\mathcal{L}_{N}$ denote the elements of $\mathcal{L}$ of homogeneous weight $N$, and let $p_N: \mathcal{L}\rightarrow \mathcal{L}_{N}$ be the projection map.

**Definition 10**. By analogy with $(\ref{UDr})$ and $(\ref{UDltN})$, define for every $r\geq 1$ a map $$D_{2r+1} : \mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2r+1}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}$$ to be $( \pi\otimes id) \circ \Delta'$, where $\Delta'=\Delta-1\otimes id$, followed by $p_{2r+1}\otimes id$. Let $$\label{DNdef} D_{<N}= \bigoplus_{3\leq 2r+1<N} D_{2r+1}\ .$$

It follows from this definition that the maps $D_{n}$, where $n=2r+1$, are derivations: $$\label{Drarederivations}
D_{n} (\xi_1 \xi_2) = (1 \otimes  \xi_1) D_{n} (\xi_2) + (1 \otimes \xi_2) D_{n}(\xi_1)  \quad \hbox{ for all } \xi_1,\xi_2 \in \mathcal{H}\ .$$ By $(\ref{defGcoproduct})$, the action of $D_n$ on $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_0;a_1,\ldots, a_N;a_{N+1})$ is given by: $$\label{mainformula}
 \sum_{p=0}^{N-n} \pi \big(I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_{p} ;a_{p+1},.\, .\,, a_{p+n}; a_{p+n+1})\big) \otimes   I^{\mathfrak{m}}(a_{0}; a_1, .\, .\,, a_{p}, a_{p+n+1}, .\, .\,,  a_N ;a_{N+1})  \ .$$ We call the sequence of consecutive elements $(a_p; a_{p+1},\ldots,a_{p+n}; a_{p+n+1})$ on the left a *subsequence of length* $n$ of the original sequence, and $( a_0;a_1, .\, .\,, a_{p}, a_{p+n+1}, .\, .\,,  a_N;a_{N+1})$ will be called the *quotient sequence*, by analogy with the Connes-Kreimer coproduct.

## Zeta elements and kernel of $D_{<N}$

**Lemma 11**. *Let $n\geq 1$. The zeta element $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)\in \mathcal{H}$ is non-zero and satisfies $$\Delta\, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1) = 1 \otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1) + \pi( \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)) \otimes 1\ .$$ Furthermore, Euler's relation $\zeta(2n)= b_n \zeta(2)^n$, where $b_n \in \mathbb Q$, holds for the $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}$'s: $$\label{zetam2n} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n) = b_n \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)^n \ .$$*

*Proof.* Consider $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N) = I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;10^{N-1};1)$. By relation $\mathbf{I0}$, its strict subsequences of length at least one are killed by $I^{\mathfrak{m}}$, and so $\Delta \, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N) = 1\otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N) + \pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N)) \otimes 1$ by $(\ref{mainformula})$. From the structure of $\mathcal{U}$, it follows that an isomorphism $\phi$ $(\ref{HisomU})$ maps $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)$ to $\alpha_n f_{2n+1}$, for some $\alpha_n \in \mathbb Q$, and $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n)$ to $\beta_n f^n_2$ for some $\beta_n\in \mathbb Q$. Taking the period map yields $\alpha_n\neq 0$, and $\beta_n=\zeta(2n) \zeta(2)^{-n}=b_n$. ◻

We can therefore normalize our choice of isomorphism $(\ref{HisomU})$ so that $$\mathcal{H}\subseteq  \mathcal{H^{MT_+}}\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow} \mathcal{U}$$ maps $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)$ to $f_{2n+1}$. By definition 8, we can therefore write: $$\label{phinormed}
\phi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N))=f_N \quad   \hbox{ for all } \quad  N\geq 2 \ .$$ In particular, $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ and $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)$, for $n\geq 1$, are algebraically independent in $\mathcal{H}$.

**Theorem 12**. *Let $N\geq 2$. The kernel of $D_{<N}$ is one-dimensional in weight $N$: $$\ker D_{<N} \cap \mathcal{H}_N = \mathbb Q\,\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N)\ .$$*

*Proof.* This follows from lemma 9, via such an isomorphism $\phi$. ◻

Note that the map $\mathcal{L}\rightarrow L$ of Lie coalgebras induced by the inclusion $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{MT}}\cong \mathcal{U}'$ is also injective, by standard results on Hopf algebras.

## Some relations between motivic multiple zeta values

Using theorem $\ref{propkerD}$ we lift relations between multiple zeta values to their motivic versions. Hereafter let $2^{\{n\}}$ denote a sequence of $n$ consecutive 2's, and let $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{0\}})=1\in \mathcal{H}$. For any word $w$ in the alphabet $\{2,3\}$, define the weight of $w$ to be $2 \deg_2 w + 3 \deg_3 w$.

**Lemma 13**. *The element $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n\}})$ is a rational multiple of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)^n$.*

*Proof.* For reasons of parity, every strict subsequence of $(0;1010\ldots 10;1)$ of odd length begins and ends in the same symbol, and corresponds to a zero motivic iterated integral by $\mathbf{I0}$. Therefore $D_{2r+1} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n\}})=0$ for all $r\geq 1$. By proposition 12 it is a multiple of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n)$, that is $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)^n$. The multiple is equal to $\zeta(2^{\{n\}})/\zeta(2)^n>0$. ◻

The coefficient in the lemma can be determined by the well-known formula $$\label{zeta222}
\zeta(2^{\{n\}}) = { \pi^{2n} \over  (2n+1)!}\ .$$ We need the following trivial observation, valid for $n\geq 1$: $$\label{12nshuffle}
\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{n\}}) =  -2 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{i\}}32^{\{n-1-i\}})\ ,$$ which follows immediately from relation $\mathbf{I2}$.

**Lemma 14**. *Let $a,b\geq 0$ and $1\leq r \leq  a+b$. Then $$D_{2r+1}\,  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}) = \pi(\xi^r_{a,b}) \otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}})\ ,$$ where $\xi^r_{a,b}$ is given by (sum over all indices $\alpha,\beta\geq0$ satisfying $\alpha+\beta+1=r$): $$\xi^r_{a,b}= \sum_{{\alpha\leq a \atop \beta \leq b}} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\alpha\}} 3 2^{\{\beta\}})  - \sum_{\alpha \leq a \atop \beta< b} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\beta\}} 3 2^{\{\alpha\}}) +\big(\mathbb{I}(b\geq r) -\mathbb{I}(a\geq r)\big)\, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{r\}}) \ ,$$ The symbol $\mathbb{I}$ denotes the indicator function.*

*Proof.* The element $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$, where $w=2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}$, is represented by the sequence $$I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;10\ldots 1{\mathbf{00}}10 \ldots 10;1)\ .$$ By parity, every subsequence of length $2r+1$ which does not straddle the subsequence ${\mathbf{00}}$ begins and ends in the same symbol. Its motivic iterated integral vanishes by $\mathbf{I0}$, so it does not contribute to $D_{2r+1}$. The remaining subsequences are of the form $$I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;\underbrace{10\ldots 10}_{\alpha}1\mathbf{00}\underbrace{10 \ldots 10}_{\beta};1)=\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\alpha\}}32^{\{\beta\}})\ ,$$ where $\alpha \leq a$, $\beta\leq b$, and $\alpha+\beta+1=r$, which gives rise to the first sum, or $$I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;\underbrace{01\ldots 01}_{\alpha}\mathbf{00}1\underbrace{01 \ldots 01}_{\beta};0)=-\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\beta\}}32^{\{\alpha\}})\ ,$$ which gives the second sum, by $\mathbf{I3}$. In this case $\beta\neq b$. Finally, we can also have $$I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;\underbrace{01\ldots 01}_{r}\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0})=-\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{r\}})\ , \hbox{ or } I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{0}; \mathbf{0}\underbrace{10\ldots 10}_{r};1)=\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{r\}})\ ,$$ which gives rise to the last two terms. The quotient sequences are the same in all cases, and equal to $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;10\ldots 10;1)$. This proves the formula. ◻

The following trivial observation follows from lemma 11: $$\label{trivob}
D_{2r+1} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(N) = \pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1)) \otimes  \delta_{N,2r+1}  \qquad N\geq 2, r\geq 1 \ ,$$ where $\delta_{i,j}$ denotes the Kronecker delta.

**Corollary 15**. *Let $w$ be a word in $\{2,3\}^{\times}$ of weight $2n+1$ which has many 2's and a single 3. Then there exist unique numbers $\alpha_i \in \mathbb Q$ such that $$\label{leveloneshape}
 \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i\,  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2i+1) \, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n-i\}}) \ .$$*

*Proof.* By induction on the weight of $w$. Suppose the result is true for $1\leq n< N$. Then for a word $w=2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}$ of weight $2N+1$, the elements $\xi^r_{a,b}$ of the previous lemma, for $1\leq r<N$, are of the form $(\ref{leveloneshape})$. In particular, there exists some rational number $\alpha_r$ such that $\xi^r_{a,b} \equiv \alpha_r\,  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1)$ modulo products. It follows that for $1\leq r < N$, $$D_{2r+1}\, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w) = \alpha_r \pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1)) \otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{N-r\}})$$ and so the left and right-hand sides of $(\ref{leveloneshape})$ have the same image under $D_{<2N+1}$ by $(\ref{Drarederivations})$ and $(\ref{trivob})$. By theorem 12 they differ by a rational multiple of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2N+1)$. ◻

The previous corollary leads us to the following definition.

**Definition 16**. Let $w$ be a word in $\{2,3\}^{\times}$ of weight $2n+1$, with a single $3$. Define the *coefficient* $c_w \in \mathbb Q$ of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$ to be the coefficient of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)$ in equation $(\ref{leveloneshape})$. By $(\ref{12nshuffle})$, we can define the coefficient $c_{12^n}$ of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{n\}})$ in the same way. It satisfies $$\label{c12id} c_{12^n}=-2 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}  c_{2^{i}32^{n-i-1}}  \ .$$ Thus for any $w \in \{2, 3\}^{\times}$ of weight $2n+1$ which contains a single 3, we have: $$\label{pizetaw}
\pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)) = c_{w} \pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1) )  \ .$$

Later on, we shall work not only with the actual coefficients $c_w\in \mathbb Q$, whose properties are described in the following section, but also with formal versions $C_w$ of these coefficients (later to be specialized to $c_w$) purely to simplify calculations.

**Lemma 17**. *For all $n\geq 1$, the coefficient $c_{12^n}$ is equal to $2(-1)^n$. We have $$\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{n\}}) = 2 \sum_{i=1}^{n}  (-1)^i \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2i+1) \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n-i\}})\ .$$*

*Proof.* Granting the motivic stuffle product formula [@Rac; @Soud] for our version of motivic multiple zeta values in which $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ is non zero, we have: $$\begin{aligned}
 \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(3)\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n-1\}})  &=  &\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{i\}}32^{\{n-1-i\}}) + \sum_{i=0}^{n-2} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{i\}} 5 2^{\{n-2-i\}}) \nonumber \\
 \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(5)\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n-2\}})  &=  &\sum_{i=0}^{n-2} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{i\}}52^{\{n-2-i\}}) + \sum_{i=0}^{n-3} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{i\}} 7 2^{\{n-3-i\}}) \nonumber \\
 \vdots \qquad \qquad & \vdots & \qquad \qquad \qquad\qquad \qquad \vdots \nonumber \\
 \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n-1)\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2) & = & \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n-1,2)+\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2,2n-1) + \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1) \ .\nonumber

\end{aligned}$$ Taking the alternating sum of each row gives the equation: $$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{i\}}32^{\{n-1-i\}}) = -2 \sum_{i=1}^{n}  (-1)^i \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2i+1) \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n-i\}})  \ .$$ This implies the lemma, by $(\ref{12nshuffle})$. Alternatively, we can use the general method for lifting relations from real multiple zeta values to their motivic versions given in [@Br]. For this, it only suffices to consider the above relations modulo products and modulo $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2)$ to obtain the coefficients of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2n+1)$, which leads to the same result. ◻

# Arithmetic of the coefficients $c_w$

The key arithmetic input is an evaluation of certain multiple zeta values which is due to Don Zagier. Using the operators $D_{<N}$ we shall lift this result to motivic multiple zetas. First, let us define for any $a,b,r\in \mathbb N$, $$A^r_{a,b} =  \binom{2r}{2a+2} \  \hbox{ and  } \ B^r_{a,b} =\bigl(1-2^{-2r}\bigr)\binom{2r}{2b+1}\ .$$ Note that $A^r_{a,b}$ (respectively $B^r_{a,b}$) only depends on $r,a$ (resp. $r,b$).

**Theorem 18**. *(Don Zagier [@Zagier]). Let $a,b\geq 0$. Then $$\zeta(2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}})=  2\,\sum_{r=1}^{a+b+1}(-1)^r (A^r_{a,b}-B^r_{a,b})\, \zeta(2r+1)\, \zeta(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}})\,\;.$$*

## Motivic version

To prove that the arithmetic formula lifts to the level of motivic multiple zeta values requires showing that it is compatible with the coaction $(\ref{fulldelta})$. This is equivalent to the following compatibility condition between the coefficients.

**Lemma 19**. *For any $a,b\geq0$, and $1\leq r\leq a+b+1$ we have $$\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{{\alpha\leq a \atop \beta \leq b}}  A^{r}_{\alpha,\beta}   - \sum_{\alpha \leq a \atop \beta< b} A^{r}_{\beta,\alpha} +    \mathbb{I}(b\geq r) -  \mathbb{I}(a\geq r)  & = & A^{r}_{a,b} \nonumber \\
  \sum_{{\alpha\leq a \atop \beta \leq b}}  B^{r}_{\alpha,\beta}   - \sum_{\alpha \leq a \atop \beta< b} B^{r}_{\beta,\alpha}   & = & B^{r}_{a,b} \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ where all sums are over sets of indices $\alpha,\beta\geq0$ satisfying $\alpha+\beta+1=r$.*

*Proof.* Exercise, using $A^{\alpha+\beta+1}_{\alpha,\beta} = A^{\alpha+\beta+1}_{\beta-1,\alpha+1}$ and $B^{\alpha+\beta+1}_{\alpha,\beta}=B^{\alpha+\beta+1}_{\beta,\alpha}$. ◻

We now show that Zagier's theorem lifts to motivic multiple zetas.

**Theorem 20**. *Let $a,b\geq 0$. Then $$\label{zl1motform} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}})=  2\,\sum_{r=1}^{a+b+1}(-1)^r (A^r_{a,b}-B^r_{a,b})\,  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1)\, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}})\;.$$ In particular, if $w=2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}$, then the coefficient $c_w$ is given by $$\label{cwformula}
c_w =   2\, (-1)^{a+b+1} \big( A^{a+b+1}_{a,b}-B^{a+b+1}_{a,b}\big)\;.$$*

*Proof.* The proof is by induction on the weight. Suppose that $(\ref{zl1motform})$ holds for all $a+b <N$ and let $a,b\geq 0$ such that $a+b=N$. Then by lemma 14, $$D_{2r+1} \, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}) =  \pi(\xi^r_{a,b})  \otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}}) \ ,$$ for $1\leq r\leq N$. The second formula in lemma 14 and $(\ref{pizetaw})$ implies that $$\pi(\xi^r_{a,b})  =  \Big( \sum_{{\alpha\leq a \atop \beta \leq b}} c_{2^\alpha 32^\beta}  - \sum_{\alpha \leq a \atop \beta< b}c_{2^{\beta}32^{\alpha}} +  c_{12^r} \mathbb{I}(b\geq r) -  c_{12^r} \mathbb{I}(a\geq r)  \Big)  \pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1))\ ,$$ where $1\leq r\leq N$ and the sum is over $\alpha, \beta\geq 0$ satisfying $\alpha+ \beta+1=r$. By induction hypothesis, and the fact that $c_{12^r}=2(-1)^r$, the term in brackets is $$2 (-1)^r\Big[ \sum_{{\alpha\leq a \atop \beta \leq b}} \Big(A^{r}_{\alpha,\beta} -B^{r}_{\alpha,\beta}\Big)  - \sum_{\alpha \leq a \atop \beta< b}\Big(A^{r}_{\beta,\alpha}-B^{r}_{\beta,\alpha}\Big) +   \mathbb{I}(b\geq r) -  \mathbb{I}(a\geq r) \Big] \ ,$$ where all $\alpha+\beta+1=r$. By lemma 19, this collapses to $$2 (-1)^r \big(  A^{r}_{a,b}- B^{r}_{a,b}  \big)\ .$$ Putting the previous expressions together, we have shown that $$D_{2r+1} \, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}}) =    2(-1)^r \big(  A^{r}_{a,b}- B^{r}_{a,b}  \big)   \pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1)) \otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}})\ .$$ It follows by $(\ref{Drarederivations})$ and $(\ref{trivob})$ that the difference $$\Theta=\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a\}}32^{\{b\}})-  2\, \sum_{r=1}^{a+b+1} (-1)^r (A^r_{a,b}-B^r_{a,b})\, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1)\,  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}})$$ satisfies $D_{2r+1}\Theta=0$ for all $r\leq a+b$. By theorem 12, there is an $\alpha\in \mathbb Q$ such that $$\Theta = \alpha \, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2a+2b+3)\ .$$ Taking the period map implies an analogous relation for the ordinary multiple zeta values. By theorem 18, the constant $\alpha$ is $2(-1)^{a+b+1}(A^{a+b+1}_{a,b}-B^{a+b+1}_{a,b})$, which completes the induction step, and hence the proof of the theorem. ◻

## $2$-adic properties of $c_w$

The coefficients $c_w$ satisfy some arithmetic properties which are crucial for the sequel, and follow immediately from theorem 20.

Let $p$ be a prime number. Recall that for any rational number $x\in \mathbb Q^{\times}$, its $p$-adic valuation $v_p(x)$ is the integer $n$ such that $x=p^n {a \over b}$, where $a,b$ are relatively prime to $p$. We set $v_p(0)=\infty$. For any word $w\in \{2,3\}^\times$, let $\widetilde{w}$ denote the same word but written in reverse order.

**Corollary 21**. *Let $w$ be any word of the form $2^a32^b$ of weight $2a+2b+3 = 2n+1$. It is obvious from formula $(\ref{cwformula})$ that $c_w \in \mathbb Z[{1\over 2}]$. Furthermore, the $c_w$ satisfy:*

1.  *$c_{w} - c_{\widetilde{w}} \in 2 \, \mathbb Z$ ,*

2.  *$v_2(c_{32^{a+b}}) \leq v_2(c_{w})\leq 0$ .*

*Proof.* Property $(1)$ follows from the symmetry $B^{a+b+1}_{a,b}=B^{a+b+1}_{b,a}$. Indeed, $$c_{2^a32^b}-c_{2^b32^a} =  \pm 2 (A^{a+b+1}_{a,b}-A^{a+b+1}_{b,a})\in 2\,  \mathbb Z\ .$$ Let $n=a+b+1$ be fixed. Clearly, $v_2(c_{2^a32^b}) = v_2 ( 2\times 2^{-2n}\times  \binom{2n}{2b+1})$, and so $$v_2(c_{2^a32^b}) = 1-2n+ v_2\big(\textstyle{\binom{2n}{2b+1}}\big)\ .$$ Writing $\binom{2n}{2b+1} = {2n \over 2b+1} \binom{2n-1}{2b}$, we obtain $$v_2(c_{2^a32^b}) = 2-2n+v_2(n) + v_2\big( \textstyle{\binom{2n-1}{2b}}\big)$$ which is $\leq 0$, and furthermore, $v_2\big( \textstyle{\binom{2n-1}{2b}}\big)$ is minimal when $b=0$. This proves $(2)$. ◻

# The level filtration and $\partial_{N,\ell}$ operators

**Definition 22**. Let $\mathcal{H}^{2,3}\subset \mathcal{H}$ denote the $\mathbb Q$-subspace spanned by $$\label{Ho23gen}
\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w) \quad  \hbox{ where } \quad  w\in \{2,3\}^{\times}\ .$$ It inherits the weight grading from $\mathcal{H}$. The weight of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$ is $2\deg_2 w+ 3\deg_3 w$.

**Definition 23**. Consider the unique map $$\rho: \{2,3\}^{\times} \longrightarrow\{0,1\}^{\times}$$ such that $\rho(2)=10$ and $\rho(3)=100$, which respects the concatenation product. The motivic iterated integral which corresponds to $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$ is $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;\rho(w);1)$.

**Lemma 24**. *The coaction $(\ref{fulldelta})$ gives a map $$\Delta: \mathcal{H}^{2,3} \longrightarrow\mathcal{A} \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}\ .$$*

*Proof.* This follows from $(\ref{defGcoproduct})$ together with the fact that $I^{\mathfrak{m}}$ vanishes on sequences which begin and end in the same symbol ($\mathbf{I0}$). Thus, for $w\in \{2,3\}^{\times}$, every quotient sequence of $(0;\rho(w);1)$ which occurs non-trivially on the right-hand side of the coaction is again of the form $(0;w';1)$, where $w'$ is a word in $10$ and $100$. ◻

##  The level filtration

**Definition 25**. Let $w\in \{2,3\}^{\times}$ be a word in the letters $2$ and $3$. We define the *level* of $w$ to be $\deg_3 w$, the number of occurrences of the letter '3' in $w$. Denote the induced increasing filtration on $\mathcal{H}^{2,3}$ by $F_{\bullet}$, i.e., $F_{\ell}\mathcal{H}^{2,3}$ is the $\mathbb Q$-vector space spanned by $$\{ \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w): w\in \{2,3\}^\times \hbox{ such that }  \deg_3 w\leq \ell\} \ .$$

The level counts the number of occurrences of the sequence '$00$' in $\rho(w)$. Alternatively, it is given by the weight minus twice the depth (number of $1$'s) in $\rho(w)$. Thus the level filtration takes even (resp. odd) values in even (resp. odd) weights. The level filtration is motivic in the following sense: $$\Delta \big( F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3} \big)  \subseteq  \mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} F_{\ell}\mathcal{H}^{2,3} \ .$$ This is because any sequence of $0$'s and $1$'s contains at least as many '$00$'s than any of its quotient sequences. It follows that the maps $D_{2r+1} :\mathcal{H}^{2,3} \rightarrow \mathcal{L}_{2r+1}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}$, where $\mathcal{L}$ is the Lie coalgebra of $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{H}/f_2\mathcal{H}$, also preserve the level filtration. In fact, more is true.

**Lemma 26**. *For all $r\geq 1,$ $$D_{2r+1} \big( F_\ell \mathcal{H}^{2,3} \big) \subseteq \mathcal{L}_{2r+1}\otimes_{\mathbb Q} F_{\ell-1} \mathcal{H}^{2,3} \ .$$*

*Proof.* Let $w\in \{2,3\}^\times$ of level $\ell$, so $\rho(w)$ contains exactly $\ell$ sequences $00$. If a subsequence of odd length of $(0;\rho(w);1)$ begins and ends in the same symbol it is killed by $I^{\mathfrak{m}}$ by $\mathbf{I0}$ and does not contribute to $D_{2r+1}$. Otherwise, it must necessarily contain at least one $00$, and so the associated quotient sequence is of strictly smaller level. ◻

Thus for all $r,\ell \geq 1$, we obtain a map $$\label{grFin5pt1}
\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} D_{2r+1}: \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3} \longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2r+1} \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}\ .$$

## The maps $\partial_{N,\ell}$

In order to simplify notations, let us define $\zeta_{2r+1}\in \mathcal{L}_{2r+1}$ by $$\zeta_{2r+1}=\pi(\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2r+1))  \ ,\quad \hbox{ where } r\geq 1\ .$$

**Lemma 27**. *Let $r, \ell \geq 1$. Then the map $(\ref{grFin5pt1})$ satisfies $$\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} D_{2r+1} \big( \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}^{2,3} \big) \subseteq \mathbb Q\, \zeta_{2r+1} \otimes_{\mathbb Q} \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1}\mathcal{H}^{2,3}\ .$$*

*Proof.* Let $w\in \{2,3\}^{\times}$ of level $\ell$, and let $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;\rho(w);1)$ be the corresponding motivic iterated integral. From the definition of $D_{2r+1}$, we have $$\label{formofdelta}
\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} D_{2r+1} (\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)) = \sum_{\gamma}  \pi(I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\gamma))\otimes \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w_\gamma)\ ,$$ where the sum is over all subsequences $\gamma$ of $(0;\rho(w);1)$ of length $2r+1$, and $w_\gamma$ is the corresponding quotient sequence. If $\gamma$ contains more than one subsequence $00$ then $w_{\gamma}$ is of level $< \ell-1$ and so does not contribute. If $\gamma$ begins and ends in the same symbol, then $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\gamma)$ is zero. One checks that $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\gamma)$ can be of four remaining types:

1.  $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;10\ldots 1\mathbf{00}10 \ldots 10;1) = \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\alpha\}}32^{\{\beta\}})$

2.  $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;01\ldots 01\mathbf{00}1 \ldots 01;0) = -\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\alpha\}}32^{\{\beta\}})$

3.  $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0}1\ldots 10;1)  = \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{r\}})$

4.  $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;01\ldots 1\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0}) = - \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{r\}})$

By corollary 15 and $(\ref{12nshuffle})$, in every case we have $\pi(I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\gamma)) \in \mathbb Q\, \zeta_{2r+1}\subset \mathcal{L}_{2r+1}$. The coefficient of $\zeta_{2r+1}$ in $\pi(I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\gamma))$ is either $\pm c_{2^\alpha32^\beta}$ or $\pm c_{12^r}$. ◻

Sending $\zeta_{2r+1}$ to $1$ gives a canonical identification of 1-dimensional vector spaces: $$\label{Qid}
\mathbb Q\, \zeta_{2r+1}  \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}  \mathbb Q\ .$$

**Definition 28**. For all $N, \ell\geq 1$, let $\partial_{N,\ell}$ be the linear map $$\begin{aligned}
\partial_{N,\ell}:  \mathrm{gr}^F_\ell \mathcal{H}^{2,3}_N & \longrightarrow& \bigoplus_{  1< 2 r+1  \leq N  } \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}_{N-2r-1} \qquad \Big( = \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}_{<N-1}\Big) \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ defined by first applying $$\bigoplus_{1 < 2r+1 \leq N}\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} D_{2r+1}\Big|_{\mathrm{gr}^F_\ell \mathcal{H}^{2,3}_N}$$ and then sending all $\zeta_{2r+1}$ to $1$ via $(\ref{Qid})$. Note that since $F_0 \mathcal{H}_0^{2,3}= \mathcal{H}_0^{2,3}$ the case $2r+1=N$ only plays a role when $\ell=1$.

Our goal is to show that the maps $\partial_{N,\ell}$ are injective for $\ell \geq 1$.

## Matrix representation for $\partial_{N,\ell}$

**Definition 29**. Let $\ell \geq 1$, and let $B_{N,\ell}$ denote the set of words $w\in \{2,3\}^{\times}$ of weight $N$ and level $\ell$, in reverse lexicographic order for the ordering $3<2$. Let $B'_{N,\ell}$ denote the set of words $w \in \{2,3\}^{\times}$ of all positive weights $<N-1$ (including the empty word) and level $\ell-1$, also in reverse lexicographic order.

If we write $N=2m+3\ell$, and if $\ell\geq 1$, then clearly $$|B_{N,\ell}| = \binom{{m+\ell}}{\ell}  = \sum_{0\leq m'\leq m} \binom{m'+\ell-1}{\ell-1} = |B'_{N,\ell}|.$$ Define a set of words $$\label{Sdef} S= \{w: w\in \{2,3\}^{\times} \hbox{ of level } 1\} \cup \{ 12^k: k\in \mathbb N\}\  .$$ Let $\ell\geq 1$, and let $w\in B_{N,\ell}$. By $(\ref{formofdelta})$ and the proof of lemma 27, $$\label{deltaf}
  \partial_{N,\ell} \,\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w) = \sum_{w'\in B'_{N,\ell}} f^w_{w'} \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w')\ ,$$ where $f^w_{w'}$ is a $\mathbb Z$-linear combination of numbers $c_{w''}\in \mathbb Q$, for $w''\in S$.

**Definition 30**. For $\ell\geq 1$, let $M_{N,\ell}$ be the matrix $(f^w_{w'})_{w\in B_{N,\ell}, w'\in B'_{N,\ell}}$, where $w$ corresponds to the rows, and $w'$ the columns.

Note that we have not yet proved that the elements $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$ for $w\in B_{N,\ell}$ or $B'_{N,\ell}$ are linearly independent. The matrix $M_{N,\ell}$ represents the map $\partial_{N,\ell}$ nonetheless.

## Formal coefficients

It is convenient to consider a formal version of the map $(\ref{deltaf})$ in which the coefficients $f^w_{w'}$ are replaced by symbols. Each matrix element $f^w_{w'}$ of $M_{N,\ell}$ is a linear combination of $c_w$, where $w\in S$. Therefore, let $\mathbb Z^{(S)}$ denote the free $\mathbb Z$-module generated by symbols $C_w$, where $w\in S$, and formally define a map $$\partial^f_{N,\ell} : \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell} \mathcal{H}_N^{2,3}  \longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{(S)} \otimes_{\mathbb Z} \mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1}\mathcal{H}^{2,3}_{<N-1}$$ from the formula $(\ref{formofdelta})$ by replacing the coefficient $\pm c_w$ of $\pi(I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\gamma))$ with its formal representative $\pm C_{w}$. Likewise, let $M^f_{N,\ell}$ be the matrix with coefficients in $\mathbb Z^{(S)}$ which is the formal version of $M_{N,\ell}$. There is a linear map $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{mudef}
\mu:\mathbb Z^{(S)} & \longrightarrow& \mathbb Q\\
C_w & \mapsto  & c_w\ . \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ Then by definition $\partial_{N,\ell} = (\mu\otimes id)  \circ \partial^f_{N,\ell}$ and $M_{N,\ell}$ is obtained from the matrix $M^f_{N,\ell}$ by applying $\mu$ to all of its entries.

# Calculation of $\partial_{N,\ell}$

Let $I \subseteq \mathbb Z^{(S)}$ be the submodule spanned by elements of the form: $$\label{Idef}
C_w-C_{\widetilde{w}} \quad  \hbox{ for }   w\in {\{2,3\} }^{\times} \hbox{ of level } 1 \ , \quad \hbox{ and } \quad  C_{12^k}   \hbox{ for }  k\in \mathbb N\ ,$$ where $\widetilde{w}$ denotes the reversal of the word $w$. We show that modulo $I$ the maps $\partial^f_{N,\ell}$ act by deconcatenation.

**Theorem 31**. *Let $w$ be a word in $\{2,3\}^{\times}$ of weight $N$, level $\ell$. Then $$\partial^f_{N,\ell} \,  \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w) \equiv \sum_{w = uv \atop \deg_3 v=1} C_v \, \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(u) \pmod I$$ where the sum is over all deconcatenations $w=uv$, where $u,v\in \{2,3\}^{\times}$, and where $v$ is of level 1, i.e., contains exactly one letter '3'.*

*Proof.* A subsequence of length $2r+1$ of the element $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;\rho(w);1)$ which corresponds to $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$ can be of the following types (compare the proof of lemma 27):

1.  The subsequence is an alternating sequence of $1$'s and $0$'s, and does not contain any consecutive $0$'s. For reasons of parity, its first and last elements are equal. Thus by $\mathbf{I0}$, this case does not contribute.

2.  The subsequence contains one set of consecutive $0$'s and is of the form $(0;w';1)$, where the inital $0$ and final $1$ are directly above the two arrows below: $$\cdots 01\underset{\uparrow}{\mathbf{0}}\mathbf{0}10 \cdots1010\underset{\uparrow}{1}0\cdots\ .$$ The word $w'$ consists of the $r+1$ symbols strictly inbetween the arrows. This subsequence corresponds to $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0}10\ldots 10;1)$ which is $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}_1(2^{\{r\}})$. The contribution is therefore $C_{12^r} \in I$. With similar notations, the case $$\cdots0\underset{\uparrow}{1} 0101 \cdots 01\mathbf{0}\underset{\uparrow}{\mathbf{0}}10\cdots$$ corresponds to $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;01\ldots 01\mathbf{0};\mathbf{0})$ and contributes $-C_{12^r} \in I$, by relation $\mathbf{I3}$.

3.  The subsequence is of one of two forms: $$\underset{a}{0}\underset{b}{1}010\cdots 1\mathbf{00}\cdots 10\underset{a'}{1}\underset{b'}{0}$$ and contains exactly one set of consecutive $0$'s. The subsequence from $a$ to $a'$ is of the form $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(0;1010\ldots 1\mathbf{00}10\ldots 10;1)$ and contributes a $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\alpha\}} 32^{\{\beta\}})$. The subsequence from $b$ to $b'$ is of the form $I^{\mathfrak{m}}(1;010\ldots 1\mathbf{00}10\ldots 101;0)$. Using relation $\mathbf{I3}$, this contributes a $(-1)^{2r+1}\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{\beta\}} 32^{\{\alpha\}})$. Thus the total contribution of these two subsequences is $$C_{2^\alpha 32^\beta } - C_{2^\beta 32^\alpha } \in I\ .$$

4.  The subsequence has at least two sets of consecutive $0$'s. This case does not contribute, since if the subsequence has level $\geq 2$, the quotient sequence has level $\leq \ell-2$ which maps to zero in $\mathrm{gr}^F_{\ell-1} \mathcal{H}^{2,3}$.

Thus every non-trivial subsequence is either of the form $(2)$, or pairs up with its immediate neighbour to the right to give a contribution of type $(3)$. The only remaining possibility is the final subsequence of $2r+1$ elements, which has no immediate right neighbour. This gives rise to a single contribution of the form $$C_v \zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(u)$$ where $w=uv$ and $v$ has weight $2r+1$. If $v$ has level strictly greater than $1$ then by the same reasoning as $(4)$ it does not contribute. This proves the theorem. ◻

**Corollary 32**. *The matrices $M^f_{N,\ell}$ modulo $I$ are upper-triangular. Every entry which lies on the diagonal is of the form $C_{32^{r-1}}$, where $r\geq 1$, and every entry lying above it in the same column is of the form $C_{2^a3 2^b}$, where $a+b+1=r$.*

*Proof.* Let $\ell \geq 1$ and consider the map $$\begin{aligned}
B'_{N,\ell-1} & \longrightarrow&  B_{N,\ell} \\
u & \mapsto & u\, 32^{\{r-1 \}} \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ where $r\geq 1$ is the unique integer such that the weight of $u\, 32^{\{r-1\}}$ is equal to $N$. This map is a bijection and preserves the ordering of both $B'_{N,\ell-1}$ and $B_{N,\ell}$. It follows from theorem 31 that the diagonal entries of $M^f_{N,\ell}$ modulo $I$ are of the form $C_{32^{r-1}}$. Now let $u \in B'_{N,\ell-1}$. All non-zero entries of $M^f_{N,\ell}$ modulo $I$ in the column indexed by $u$ lie in the rows indexed by $u 2^{\{a\}} 3 2^{\{ b\}}$, where $a+b=r-1$. Since $$u 2^{\{a\}} 3 2^{\{ b\}} < u \, 32^{\{r-1 \}}\ ,$$ it is upper-triangular, and the entry in row $u 2^{\{a\}} 3 2^{\{ b\}}$ and column $u$ is $C_{2^a3 2^b}$. ◻

# Proof of the main theorem

## $p$-adic lemma

We need the following elementary lemma.

**Lemma 33**. *Let $p$ be a prime, and let $v_p$ denote the $p$-adic valuation. Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a square $n\times n$ matrix with rational coefficients such that $$\begin{aligned}
 \label{padiccond}
&(i)&  v_p(a_{ij}) \geq 1 \quad \hbox{ for all } i>j \\
&(ii)&   v_p(a_{jj})=  \underset{i}{\min}\{v_p(a_{ij})\}\leq 0  \quad \hbox{ for all }  j \ . \nonumber
\end{aligned}$$ Then $A$ is invertible.*

*Proof.* We show that the determinant of $A$ is non-zero. The determinant is an alternating sum of products of elements of $A$, taken one from each row and column. Any such monomial $m\neq 0$ has $k$ terms on or above the diagonal, in columns $j_1,\ldots, j_k$, and $n-k$ terms strictly below the diagonal. By $(i)$ and $(ii)$ its $p$-adic valuation is $$v_p(m)  \geq   (n-k) + \sum_{r=1}^k v_p(a_{j_rj_r})$$ If $m$ is not the monomial $m_0$ in which all terms lie on the diagonal, then $k<n$, and $$v_p(m) >   \sum_{i=1}^n v_p(a_{ii}) =v_p(m_0)\ .$$ It follows that $v_p(\det(A)) =  v_p(m_0)=\sum_{i=1}^n v_p(a_{ii}) \leq 0 <\infty$, so $\det(A) \neq 0$. ◻

*Remark 34*. Another way to prove this lemma is simply to scale each column of the matrix $A$ by a suitable power of $p$ so that $(i)$ remains true, and so that the diagonal elements have valuation exactly equal to $0$. The new matrix has $p$-integral coefficients by $(ii)$, is upper-triangular mod $p$, and is invertible on the diagonal.

**Theorem 35**. *For all $N, \ell\geq 1$, the matrices $M_{N,\ell}$ are invertible.*

*Proof.* We show that $M_{N,\ell}$ satisfies the conditions $(\ref{padiccond})$ for $p=2$. The entries of $M_{N,\ell}$ are deduced from those of $M^f_{N,\ell}$ by applying the map $\mu$ of $(\ref{mudef})$. It follows from corollary 21 (1) and lemma 17 that the generators $(\ref{Idef})$ of $I$ map to even integers under $\mu$ i.e., $$\mu(I) \subset 2\, \mathbb Z\  .$$ By corollary $\ref{coruppertri}$, this implies that $M_{N,\ell}$ satisfies $(i)$. Property $(ii)$ follows from corollary 21 $(2)$, since the diagonal entries of $M^f_{N,\ell} \mod I$ are $C_{32^k}$, $k\in\mathbb N$. ◻

## Proof of the main theorem

**Theorem 36**. *The elements $\{\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w): w\in \{2,3\}^{\times}\}$ are linearly independent.*

*Proof.* By induction on the level. The elements of level zero are of the form $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(2^{\{n\}})$ for $n\geq 0$, which by lemma 13 are linearly independent. Now suppose that $$\{\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w): w\in \{2,3\}^\times \ , \quad w \hbox{ of level } = \ell\}$$ are independent. Since the weight is a grading on the motivic multiple zeta values, we can assume that any non-trivial linear relation between $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$, for $w\in \{2,3\}^\times$ of level $\ell+1$ is of homogeneous weight $N$. By theorem 35, the map $\partial_{N,\ell+1}$ is injective and therefore gives a non-trivial relation of strictly smaller level, a contradiction. Thus $\{\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w): w\in \{2,3\}^\times \hbox{ of level }\ell+1\}$ are linearly independent, which completes the induction step. The fact that the operator $D_{\leq N}$ strictly decreases the level (lemma 26), and that its level-graded pieces $\partial_{N,\ell}$ are injective implies that there can be no non-trivial relations between elements $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$ of different levels. ◻

It follows that $$\label{lowerbound} \dim \mathcal{H}_N^{2,3}  =  \#\{w\in \{2,3\}^\times  \hbox{ of weight } N\} =  d_N\ ,$$ where $d_N$ is the dimension of $\mathcal{U}_N$ $(\ref{enumeration})$. The inclusions $$\mathcal{H}^{2,3}\subseteq \mathcal{H}\subseteq \mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$$ are therefore all equalities, since their dimensions in graded weight $N$ are equal. The equality $\mathcal{H}^{2,3}=\mathcal{H}$ implies the following corollary:

**Corollary 37**. *Every motivic multiple zeta value $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(a_1,\ldots, a_n)$ is a $\mathbb Q$-linear combination of $\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w)$, for $w\in\{2,3\}^\times$.*

This implies conjecture 2. The equality $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H^{MT_+}}$ implies conjecture 1.

# Remarks

By adapting the above argument, one could show that the elements $$\{\zeta^{ \mathfrak{m}}(w): \hbox{ where } w \hbox{ is a Lyndon word in } \{2,3\}^\times\}$$ are algebraically independent in $\mathcal{H}$. That their periods should be algebraically independent was conjectured in [@DataMine]. It might also be interesting to try to use the matrices $M_{N,\ell}$ to compute the multiplication law on the basis $(\ref{Hbasis})$.

The geometric meaning of theorem 20 is not clear. The term $(1-2^{-2r}) \zeta(2r+1)$ which comes from $B^{a,b}_r$ can be interpreted as an alternating sum and suggests that the formula should be viewed as an identity between motivic iterated integrals on $\mathbb P^1\backslash \{0,\pm 1,\infty\}$. It would be interesting to find a direct motivic proof of theorem 20 along these lines. Apart from the final step of §7.2, the only other place where we use the structure of the category $\mathcal{MT}(\mathbb Z)$ is in theorem 20. A proof of theorem 20 using standard relations would give a purely combinatorial proof that $\dim \mathcal{H}_N \geq d_N$.

The argument in this paper could be dualized to take place in Ihara's algebra, using his pre-Lie operator ([@DG], $(5.13.5)$) instead of Goncharov's formula $(\ref{defGcoproduct})$. This sheds some light on the appearance of the deconcatenation coproduct in theorem 31.

Finally, we should point out that the existence of a different explicit basis for multiple zeta values was apparently announced many years ago by J. Ecalle.
